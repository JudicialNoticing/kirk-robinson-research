# Tier 1 Source Acquisition Roadmap

**State of Utah v. Tyler James Robinson — Case No. 251403576, Fourth District Court (Provo), Judge Tony F. Graf Jr.**

*Compiled July 13, 2026*

---

## Scope

Tier 1 in this project = **sworn testimony and primary court filings**. This roadmap lists every identified T1 source that exists or will foreseeably exist, the concrete steps to obtain it, feasibility, and — where applicable — which registered open questions it could resolve. Official press statements (e.g., the FBI's case-updates page) are deliberately excluded here: they are Tier 2 (named official statements) under the framework, however useful.

A general access note: Utah's Xchange system makes all public district court documents filed electronically since July 1, 2010 available online by subscription, with free public terminals at most district courthouses and the Utah State Law Library in Salt Lake City ([utcourts.gov](https://www.utcourts.gov/en/court-records-publications/records/xchange.html), [court records guide](https://www.utcourts.gov/en/court-records-publications/records/coris.html)). Remote acquisition (subscription, email/phone requests) is possible for nearly everything below; the free-terminal route requires being in Utah.

## A. Accessible now — no gatekeeper

**1. DocumentCloud case repository.** A maintained collection described as comprehensive official court filings, docket records, and public documents from Case No. 251403576 ([documentcloud.org, project 224251](https://www.documentcloud.org/projects/224251-charlie-kirk-murder-case-and-tyler-robinson/)).
- *Steps:* Browse and download directly; free.
- *Verification discipline:* These are mirrored copies. Authenticate each PDF against the official docket (source 2) before citing as T1; cite by document title, filing date, and case number, noting "via DocumentCloud mirror."
- *Resolves:* The general primary-source gap — replaces news-mediated citations for the charging information, probable cause statement, and motions practice. Enables verbatim comparison of the confession note's wording across filings (the registered tense-inconsistency question).

**2. Xchange docket and public filings.** The authoritative docket for 251403576 and electronic copies of every public filing.
- *Steps:* Create a subscription account at Xchange (fees apply; exact current pricing on the signup page), or use a free public terminal in Utah; search by case number.
- *Yields:* Charging information (Sept. 16, 2025), probable cause statement, the full motions record (media-access litigation, camera motions, the disqualification motion, hearsay motion and Graf's June 22, 2026 ruling, exhibit-tier orders), and every future filing.
- *Note:* Graf's March 13, 2026 ruling rejected a defense bid to seal several filings and established that closure motions require notice to media, with a presumptive public right of access ([St. George News](https://www.stgeorgeutah.com/news/judge-denies-request-to-seal-filings-in-tyler-robinson-case-weighing-media-access/article_b722f315-c962-413e-86ca-9ae9b92fd868.html), [KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51463583/tyler-robinson-back-in-court-to-argue-against-public-access-to-his-motions)) — the docket should therefore be monitored for closure motions as an early-warning system.

**3. Redacted transcript and audio of the closed October 2025 hearing.** Graf ordered the 80-page transcript released on Dec. 29, 2025 with only 246 words redacted, along with the audio ([KUTV](https://kutv.com/news/local/tyler-robinson-expected-in-court-as-attorneys-discuss-media-access)).
- *Steps:* Pull from the docket/Xchange; copies also circulated via media (a redacted hearing transcript was published by at least one national outlet).
- *Resolves (potentially):* If this closed hearing concerned the recorded Twiggs interviews or suppression issues, it may bear directly on the registered Miranda-timestamp conflict and the interview-admissibility fight. Confirm the hearing's subject from the transcript itself.

**4. Archived video of the July 6–10, 2026 preliminary hearing.** Graf permitted cameras; the proceedings were livestreamed in full via a court-sanctioned pool feed and carried by multiple outlets. This is sworn testimony, verbatim.
- *Steps:* Locate archived full-day streams (multiple outlets carried the entirety; at least one streamed all five days on YouTube).
- *Caveats:* A pool feed is not the certified record — use it for verbatim quotation with timestamps, but reconcile against the certified transcript (source 5) for citation-locking. Graf's late-week order restricted courtroom display of most exhibits, so the video will not show all exhibits even where testimony about them is audible.
- *Resolves:* Exact wording of Hull, Bagley, Faumuina, Davis, and the defense-called FBI/ATF experts — including the Dremel testimony (currently single-outlet), the item-level DNA statements, and the precise likelihood-ratio phrasings read into the record.

## B. Obtainable now by request — forms, fees, short waits

**5. Certified transcript of the preliminary hearing.** The official verbatim record. Both parties told Graf they want the transcript before briefing, so transcription is already in progress; the state's July 28 brief deadline implies counsel availability by then.
- *Steps:* Submit a transcript request through the Fourth District Court's transcript-request process (Utah hearings are recorded and transcribed by court-approved transcribers for per-page fees; the request pathway is on utcourts.gov). Ask the clerk whether any portions are sealed under the exhibit-tier order.
- *Resolves:* This is the single highest-value acquisition on the list. It resolves at the record level: item-level DNA splits and the contested percentage reporting; the "consistent with" vs. "had been fired by" cartridge-casing language as actually testified; the FBI/ATF division of labor; the Dremel claim; and the Zinn detention-timing discrepancy as testified by Bagley.

**6. Hearing audio recordings.** Utah district courts are courts of record; hearings are recorded, and utcourts.gov maintains a "Request Audio Recording" process.
- *Steps:* Submit the audio request form to the Fourth District clerk with case number and hearing dates; nominal media fee. Subject to closure orders, but the October precedent (source 3) shows the court releasing even closed-hearing audio with minimal redaction.

**7. Unsealed search warrant affidavits and returns.** Multiple warrant packets have been unsealed and reported (e.g., coverage of warrants detailing post-shooting messages).
- *Steps:* Request from the issuing court(s) via the clerk; check the DocumentCloud repository first, as reporters frequently deposit these.
- *Resolves:* Warrant *returns* itemize seized property — the most direct documentary check on the Dremel, shooting targets, and ammunition claims, independent of testimony. Affidavits also give investigators' sworn chronology, useful against the arrest/booking-time conflicts.

**8. State v. George Zinn case file.** Fourth District (Judge Thomas Low): information, probable cause affidavit, plea statement, and sentencing order.
- *Steps:* Xchange search by defendant name; request documents.
- *Resolves:* Discrepancy Register item 4 (Bagley's ~30 seconds vs. police reports' ~2 minutes for the Zinn detention) at the primary-record level, plus the sworn basis for the diversion-of-resources account.

**9. Utah County Jail booking record for Robinson.**
- *Steps:* Records request to the Utah County Sheriff's Office (booking information is generally public; GRAMA request if needed).
- *Resolves:* The surrender-to-booking window (Register item 7) and the memo'd arrest/booking-time conflicts, by fixing the official booking timestamp.

## C. Contingent — will exist or may be released; monitor and then pull

**10. Probable cause briefs.** State's brief due **July 28**; defense response **Aug. 11**; state rebuttal **Aug. 18** ([KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51596294/tyler-robinson-preliminary-hearing-expected-to-wrap-up-friday)).
- *Steps:* Docket pull on each date (watch for closure motions, which require media notice).
- *Value:* Each side's evidence characterization with record citations — effectively an adversarial index to the preliminary hearing record, ideal for citation-locking the evidence file.

**11. Graf's probable cause ruling.** Following the four-hour oral argument set for **Sept. 1, 2026**.
- *Steps:* Docket pull when issued.
- *Value:* Judicial findings on the sufficiency of the evidence — the first adjudicative T1 assessment of the state's narrative.

**12. Admitted exhibits under the tier system.** Graf is deciding per exhibit whether items are admitted, displayed in court, and/or released publicly; the Kirk family has formally requested public release of the evidence ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/11/us/charlie-kirk-tyler-robinson-hearing-takeaways)).
- *Potential yields:* The FBI DNA report(s) (item-level results and likelihood ratios); the ATF firearms and DNA reports (the cartridge-conclusion language; the ATF reporting-ceiling issue); the surveillance compilation; scene, rifle, and cartridge photographs; the medical examiner's report; the recorded Twiggs interviews (the April interview was played in court).
- *Steps:* Monitor the docket for exhibit-release orders, then request copies from the clerk; expect redactions.
- *Resolves:* Nearly every remaining forensic open question, at the source-document level rather than the testimony-about-documents level.

**13. Twiggs-related filings.** Immunity paperwork, the interstate witness litigation (Graf denied compelling travel), and motions concerning the two recorded law-enforcement interviews.
- *Steps:* Docket search; where classified private, the March 13 framework provides the mechanism to seek reclassification (and requires the parties to notify media of closure attempts).

**14. Suppression/Miranda filings.** The defense filing containing the Miranda-warning timestamp that conflicts with the official surrender chronology.
- *Steps:* Docket search for suppression-related motions; cross-check against source 3 (the October closed hearing) and source 9 (booking record) to attempt reconciliation.

## D. Long-shot or post-adjudication

**15. FBI records via FOIA (eFOIPA).** Expect denial under Exemption 7(A) (pending enforcement proceedings) while the prosecution is live. File post-adjudication; the eventual file would include the underlying lab bench notes and 302s.

**16. Utah DPS/SBI investigative records via GRAMA.** Protected during an active prosecution; appeals possible but unlikely to succeed now. Viable after final judgment.

**17. UVU records via GRAMA.** As a public university, UVU is GRAMA-subject. Investigation-linked materials (surveillance video) will be withheld, but **event security-planning records** may be partially obtainable and would test the "six officers, no metal detectors, no pre-event security meeting" testimony against contemporaneous documents.

**18. Medical examiner's autopsy report.** Statutorily restricted in Utah (next-of-kin and enumerated requesters); the realistic public path is its status as a court exhibit under the tier system (source 12).

**19. The trial record.** If Robinson is bound over and tried: full trial transcripts, the admitted-exhibit set, jury instructions, and verdict forms — the eventual definitive T1 corpus.

## Standing practice notes

1. **Mirror discipline:** Anything pulled from DocumentCloud or media-posted PDFs gets authenticated against the Xchange docket entry before it enters the citation-locked file.
2. **Citation format for T1:** case number, document title, filing date, docket entry (or hearing date + transcript page:line), plus acquisition route.
3. **Docket watch cadence:** July 28, Aug. 11, Aug. 18 (briefs), Sept. 1 (oral argument), then the probable cause ruling. Closure motions must be noticed to media under the March 13 framework, so they will surface on the docket before anything disappears.
4. **Pool video vs. certified transcript:** quote from video with timestamps provisionally; lock citations only against the certified transcript.
