## 0. The governing access-law framework (Utah) — read first

Getting the *channel* right matters, because Utah routes court records and agency records through **two separate legal regimes**:

- **Court case records** — dockets, filed motions/briefs, **admitted exhibits, and hearing transcripts** — are governed by the **Utah Code of Judicial Administration (CJA) Rule 4-202 series**, obtained through the **court/clerk**, *not* through GRAMA. Court case records are presumptively public. Sources: https://legacy.utcourts.gov/rules/view.php?type=ucja&rule=4-202.02 ; https://www.rcfp.org/open-courts-compendium/utah/
- **Executive-agency investigative records** — reports, body-cam, lab submissions, and files held by the **State Bureau of Investigation / Utah DPS, UVU PD, Washington County Sheriff's Office** — are governed by **GRAMA (Utah Code Title 63G, Ch. 2)**, obtained by a GRAMA request to that agency.
- **Federal lab work product (FBI, ATF)** in this case exists as **state-court exhibits.** The correct venue is the **state court record**, not federal FOIA (see §6-G for why FOIA is the wrong channel here).

**THE CRITICAL CATCH — CJA 4-202.02(2)(J):** Exhibits are presumptively public court records, **"but the judge may regulate or deny access to ensure the integrity of the exhibit, a fair trial or interests favoring closure."** Source: https://legacy.utcourts.gov/rules/view.php?type=ucja&rule=4-202.02

Judge Graf has already exercised exactly this fair-trial gatekeeping repeatedly (held a prosecutor in contempt over pretrial publicity; sealed the handwritten note; sealed Mitchell's witness statement; restricted the enhanced video to the gallery only). **Assume the sensitive DNA exhibits may be access-regulated under 4-202.02(2)(J), and that obtaining raw lab reports before trial may require a motion under CJA 4-202.04 with notice to the parties — which the State and defense both have fair-trial incentives to oppose.** This is the single largest barrier in this roadmap and it is realistic, not hypothetical.

## 2. Channel playbook

### A. Certified transcript (T-1) — CJA 4-202.04
The parties themselves requested the transcript before briefing, so it **will be produced.** A member of the public may order a copy of a transcript of a public hearing from the assigned court reporter/transcriber via the clerk. Expect a per-page fee and a multi-week production window. This is the **single highest-value authoritative source** — it resolves the largest cluster of open items at once. Order all five days.

### B. Court docket & filed exhibits (T-2, T-3, T-4, T-6, T-11) — XChange / online case search
- **Utah's free "online case search"** gives the docket, party/hearing data, and index.
- **XChange** (Utah's subscription tier) gives fuller access to filed documents. *Subscription still outstanding per prior notes — this is the enabling step for B, T-5, T-6, T-11.*
- Admitted exhibits are presumptively public **but subject to 4-202.02(2)(J) fair-trial regulation** (see §0). For any exhibit that is access-regulated, sealed, or protected, a **CJA 4-202.04 motion to access** (with notice to parties) is required, and the judge balances transparency against fair-trial interests under 4-202. Sources: https://legacy.utcourts.gov/rules/view.php?type=ucja&rule=4-202.04 ; https://legacy.utcourts.gov/rules/view.php?type=ucja&rule=4-202.02

### C. The probable-cause briefs (T-5) — the near-term shortcut
State opening due **July 28**, defense response **Aug. 11**, State reply **Aug. 18**. These are public docket filings (unless sealed) and will **quote the transcript and exhibits verbatim** to argue probable cause. This is the fastest, cheapest route to authoritative exhibit language on the contested items — **watch the docket on those three dates.**

### D. GRAMA to executive agencies (T-9) — Utah Code Title 63G, Ch. 2
For SBI/DPS chain-of-custody, UVU PD, and UCSO investigative records. **Realistic expectation: denial or deferral.** Records of an ongoing law-enforcement proceeding may be classified **protected** under Utah Code **§ 63G-2-305**, and agencies routinely withhold active-prosecution investigative files. File anyway to (a) create a paper record of the request/denial and (b) capture anything releasable, but do **not** treat this as a primary path for time-sensitive items. Standard GRAMA response window is generally ~10 business days; fees may apply. Note the appeal path if denied.

### E. Broadcast / livestream video (T-7) — immediate, unofficial
The hearing was **televised live** (Graf permitted cameras under the judiciary's media-coverage rules). A pool video record exists. **Transcribing the relevant testimony from the broadcast yields verbatim language now**, ahead of the certified transcript — with the explicit caveat that a self-transcription is **unofficial** and must be reconciled against T-1 when it arrives. Note: the *enhanced/zoomed* surveillance compilation was shown to the gallery **only**, not the media feed, so it will not be on the broadcast.

### F. Pool photography (T-8)
Named pool photographers (AP/Spenser Heaps; SL Tribune/Trent Nelson; Deseret/Tess Crowley) captured displayed exhibits — the rifle-in-towel, the "Catch!" and "TEST SHOT" engraved casings, and the text-message photo. Where an exhibit's *content* was displayed in open court, the published photo is a legitimate imagery source for that content. Useful for the engraving/ammunition items; not a substitute for report text.

### G. Federal FOIA (FBI/ATF) — **wrong channel; document why, then skip for now**
The FBI and ATF reports are **state-court exhibits**; the state court record is the proper venue. A federal FOIA request to FBI/ATF during an **active prosecution** would almost certainly be denied under **FOIA Exemption 7(A)** (law-enforcement records whose disclosure could interfere with ongoing proceedings). Log this reasoning so the channel isn't naively re-attempted; revisit only post-disposition.

## 6. Request-drafting checklists

**CJA 4-202.04 request to access a case record (court records):**
- Case caption + number (State v. Robinson, 251403576, 4th Dist.).
- Precisely identify the record (e.g., "State's Exhibit ___, FBI Laboratory DNA report authored by A. Bakker, items 7(1) and 7(2)/8(1); and the certified transcript of the July 7–10, 2026 preliminary hearing").
- State requester status (member of public) and that court case records are presumptively public under CJA 4-202.02(1).
- Acknowledge exhibits may be regulated under 4-202.02(2)(J); if the record is non-public, frame as a motion to access with notice to parties per 4-202.04.
- Specify copy format; accept fee terms (4-202.09).

**GRAMA request (executive agencies — SBI/DPS, UVU PD, UCSO):**
- Cite Utah Code § 63G-2-201; identify agency + records (e.g., "evidence intake and chain-of-custody records for the towel, item 7(1), and the screwdriver, item 8(1), UVU shooting investigation, Sept. 2025").
- Request fee waiver if applicable (public interest).
- Anticipate § 63G-2-305 protected-record denial; preserve the § 63G-2-403 appeal path and note the denial for the register.

## 7. Verification protocol once a primary is obtained

For each acquired document, before promoting a claim from **[T1 via T3]** to **T1** in the companion file:
1. Confirm the **item/exhibit number** matches the FBI item-numbering (7(1)=towel, 8(1)=screwdriver) — do **not** conflate with any ATF "Exhibit 7.1" scheme (open Q8).
2. Record the **exact statistic and its hypothesis pair** verbatim (LR value + numerator/denominator hypotheses), not a paraphrase.
3. Note whether the figure is a **measured value or a reporting cap** (ATF 10¹² ceiling).
4. Separate **testified fact** from **attorney characterization** (e.g., Burt's "subjectively changed" vs. the report's stated basis).
5. Update the companion file's confidence ledger (§8) and register (§7), and log the promotion in its Revision Log (§10) with the source document and date.
6. Preserve any *new* conflict discovered against reporting as a fresh register entry rather than overwriting.
