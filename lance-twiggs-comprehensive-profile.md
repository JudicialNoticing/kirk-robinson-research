# Lance Twiggs: Comprehensive Evidentiary Profile
## *State of Utah v. Tyler James Robinson* — Research Document

**Compiled:** July 10, 2026 (Day 5 of the preliminary hearing) · **Version 1.1** — audited against fresh verification searches same day; see Revision Log (§13)
**Scope:** All publicly documented facts concerning Lance Twiggs — Tyler Robinson's roommate and romantic partner — drawn from charging documents, unsealed search warrants, preliminary hearing testimony (July 6–10, 2026), law enforcement statements, and media reporting from September 2025 through July 2026.

---

## Methodological Note on Sourcing

This document distinguishes among four tiers of sourcing reliability:

1. **Official court records and sworn testimony** — charging documents, unsealed search warrants, judicial rulings, and testimony given at the preliminary hearing (including Twiggs' recorded interviews as played in court).
2. **On-the-record statements by named officials** — Utah Gov. Spencer Cox, Utah County Attorney Jeff Gray, FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino, testifying agents.
3. **Named but non-official sources** — relatives, friends, and neighbors quoted in press reports.
4. **Anonymous, tabloid, or uncorroborated claims** — flagged explicitly as unverified.

Most court-record content below is accessed through news reporting of proceedings and filings rather than the primary documents themselves. Where official sources conflict with each other or with media accounts, the discrepancy is flagged rather than resolved.

A critical framing point: **Twiggs has never spoken publicly.** He has given no interviews and issued no statements to media. Everything attributed to "what Twiggs has said" comes exclusively from his two recorded law enforcement interviews (September 12, 2025 and April 20, 2026), portions of which were played in court on July 9, 2026, and from his text messages as reproduced in court filings.

## 1. Identity and Background

### 1.1 Established biographical facts

- **Name:** Lance S. Twiggs. Court filings by Robinson's defense state that Twiggs' chosen name is **"Luna"** and use she/her pronouns; Judge Tony Graf's rulings and the prosecution use "Lance Twiggs" and he/him pronouns (KUER, June 22, 2026). Robinson's handwritten note, shown in full in court on July 9, 2026, is addressed to **"Luna."** Media reporting overwhelmingly uses "Lance Twiggs" and he/him; this document follows the court records' usage for traceability, without taking a position on the dispute.
- **Age:** 22 at the time of the shooting (September 2025); reported as born ca. 2002–2003.
- **Hometown:** St. George, Utah. Raised in what relatives and Gov. Cox described as a conservative, Latter-day Saint (Mormon) family.
- **Education/occupation:** Reportedly a gifted pianist in school and a high academic achiever ("straight A" per friends quoted by the Times of India); a Facebook profile listed Utah Tech University; described himself online as a "digital creator" and aspiring professional gamer. No authoritative confirmation of degree completion exists in public records (Factually.co synthesis, Dec. 3, 2025).

### 1.2 Gender identity

- Utah Gov. Spencer Cox stated on NBC's *Meet the Press* (Sept. 14, 2025): "we can confirm that that roommate is a boyfriend who is transitioning from male to female" (Newsweek).
- Robinson's mother told investigators, per the charging document, that her son "began to date his roommate, a biological male who was transitioning genders" (Al Jazeera; CNN; CBS).
- Online researchers noted a gaming-platform display-name change from "Lance" to "Luna" — reported with varying specificity (Times of India; the Factually.co synthesis renders it as "a Steam or Stream display name change," reflecting source-level uncertainty about the platform). Consistent with, but independent of, the court-record confirmation via the note's salutation.
- Twiggs' grandfather, asked by the Daily Mail in September 2025, declined to confirm whether Twiggs was transgender; another relative called Twiggs the "black sheep" of the family and said she "wouldn't be surprised" if he were transitioning (thenewsgrail.com; WION).

**Analytical note:** The identity question became politically charged because Kirk was shot while answering audience questions about mass shootings, in an exchange that had immediately referenced transgender shooters (Washington Post timeline: "answering a question from an audience member about responsibility for mass shootings"). No official source — prosecutor, court, or investigator — has asserted any connection between Twiggs' identity and Robinson's motive. Utah County Attorney Jeff Gray, asked directly at the Sept. 16, 2025 press conference whether "transgender issues played a role in the motivations," declined to go beyond the charging document (The Advocate).

### 1.3 Family background and estrangement — contested territory

- Multiple relatives told reporters that Twiggs was estranged from his family. A relative told Fox News Digital (Jan. 2026) that Twiggs "struggled with drug and alcohol abuse, was addicted to video games, and was grappling with his gender identity," and that these issues "fueled repeated conflicts with family members" (Fox News, Jan. 15, 2026). A widely circulated Quora-tier claim that his father "kicked him out at 18" traces to this same family-sourced narrative and is not officially corroborated.
- Friends pushed back strongly. Coverage in the Times of India ("He's a good kid; straight A... left family over faith") and Hindustan Times ("He doesn't deserve this... he took care of people," Sept. 25, 2025) quoted loved ones defending Twiggs as kind and hardworking and denying extensive drug use; some framed the family split as driven by faith conflict rather than misconduct.
- **Status:** The estrangement itself is consistently reported across friendly and hostile sources. The *cause* (substance abuse vs. faith/identity conflict) is contested, maps directly onto who is speaking (relatives vs. friends), and is unadjudicated. No court record addresses it.

- Twiggs said he **met Robinson in person for the first time in 2023**, when Robinson moved in as a new roommate at the St. George apartment (Fossil Hills complex, per September 2025 reporting). *(Labeled inference: the "in person for the first time" phrasing in the liveblog account may imply prior online acquaintance; no source confirms or dates any such contact.)*

Early reporting described a three-bedroom unit with a third roommate at some point (thenewsgrail.com).
- A neighbor told reporters in September 2025 that he had seen the two holding hands and kissing (thenewsgrail.com — tier 3 source).

### 2.2 Official confirmations of the romantic relationship

- **Gov. Cox** (Sept. 14, 2025, *Meet the Press* and CNN): confirmed the roommate was Robinson's boyfriend, "very cooperative," and "had no idea that this was happening."
- **FBI Deputy Director Dan Bongino** (Megyn Kelly Show): confirmed "Lance Twiggs" as "the boyfriend's name," citing forensic and digital evidence plus family/friend statements indicating "a relationship here that may have been beyond platonic" (Newsweek).

By the time of the 2026 proceedings, courts and media uniformly describe Twiggs as Robinson's **former** partner; no public record establishes exactly when or how the relationship ended, beyond Twiggs' relocation out of state.

## 3. September 10–12, 2025: The Critical 72 Hours

This is the core of Twiggs' evidentiary significance. The account below merges the charging documents (Sept. 16, 2025), unsealed search warrants (April 2026), and Twiggs' interview statements as played July 9, 2026.

### 3.1 The morning of September 10 (Twiggs' account)

Twiggs told prosecutors Robinson **left the apartment around 4–5 a.m. on Sept. 10**, saying he had a long drive for work. Twiggs said he **did not hear from Robinson again until about 11 p.m.** that night (NewsNation, July 9, 2026).

### 3.2 The auto-text and the note

- Robinson texted Twiggs: **"Drop what you are doing, look under my keyboard"** (charging documents). The full note revealed July 9 states Robinson "set an auto text" before leaving — indicating the message may have been pre-scheduled rather than sent live. The precise send time has been reported as roughly 11 p.m. per Twiggs' interview account, hours after the 12:23 p.m. shooting.
- Under the keyboard, Twiggs found a handwritten note. **Two versions of its key line exist in official records:**
  - **Charging documents (Sept. 16, 2025):** "I had the opportunity to take out Charlie Kirk and **I'm going to take it**."
  - **Full note as displayed in court (July 9, 2026)** and described in unsealed April 2026 search warrants: "Luna, If you are reading this per my text, then I am so sorry. I left the house this morning on a mission, and set an auto text. I am likely dead, or facing a lengthy prison sentence. I had the opportunity to take out Charlie Kirk, and **I took it**. I don't know if I will/have succeeded, but I had hoped to make it home to you. I wish we could have lived in a world where this did not feel necessary. I wish I could have stayed for you and lived our lives together. I lack the words to express how much I love you, and how very much you mean to me... Please try and find joy in this life. I love you always, Tyler." (ABC4, April 2026; The Hill, July 9, 2026)
  - **Flag:** The tense discrepancy ("I'm going to take it" vs. "I took it") between the September charging-document quotation and the physical note as later displayed has not been publicly explained. The most economical explanation is imprecise quotation in the initial filing, but this is inference, not record — and it is complicated by the fact that the charging document itself states police possessed **a photo of the note**, which would have permitted exact transcription. An alternative is that the filing quoted the note as relayed secondhand (e.g., via Twiggs' account) rather than from the photo. Neither explanation is established.
  - **Minor transcription variants** also exist across renderings of the same note: "**set** an auto text" (ABC4, from the warrant affidavit) vs. "**sent** an auto text" (KSL and Deseret News, quoting the same affidavit; The Hill's July 9 courtroom rendering); and "a world where this **did not feel** necessary" (AP; ABC4) vs. "**was not** necessary" (The Hill, July 9). The auto-text point in §3.2 holds under either reading; resolution path for exact wording: the affidavit itself and the courtroom exhibit.
- **Fate of the note:** Twiggs told prosecutors he **photographed the note and left it in Robinson's room** (NewsNation, July 9, 2026). The charging documents state police found **a photo of the note**; they "did not say if the note itself was destroyed" (CBS, Sept. 16, 2025). Unsealed warrants and July 9 courtroom imagery establish the **original was partially burned and was recovered and pieced together by forensics** — an image of burned paper was displayed in court July 9 (Washington Examiner; ZeroHedge summarizing unsealed documents). **Who burned the note, when, and where has not been publicly established.** No official source has accused Twiggs of destroying it, and prosecutors' witness-tampering counts against Robinson concern the text messages, not the note.

### 3.3 The text exchange (as reproduced in charging documents)

After reading the note, per the filings:

- Twiggs: "What?????????????? You're joking, right????"
- Robinson: "I am still ok my love, but am stuck in orem for a little while longer yet. Shouldn't be long until I can come home, but I gotta grab my rifle still. To be honest I had hoped to keep this secret till I died of old age. I am sorry to involve you."
- Twiggs: "you weren't the one who did it right????"
- Robinson: "I am, I'm sorry."
- Twiggs: "I thought they caught the person?"
- Robinson: "no, they grabbed some crazy old dude, then interrogated someone in similar clothing. I had planned to grab my rifle from my drop point shortly after, but most of that side of town got locked down." *(The "crazy old dude" is understood to refer to George Zinn — connecting to the Zinn thread researched previously; note this is Robinson's characterization, not an official finding.)*
- Twiggs asked **why**. Robinson: "I had enough of his hatred. Some hate can't be negotiated out."
- Twiggs: "How long have you been planning this?" Robinson: "A bit over a week, I believe."
- Robinson on the weapon: "If I am able to grab my rifle unseen, I will have left no evidence... I'm worried what my old man would do if I didn't bring back grandpas rifle... idek if it had a serial number, but it wouldn't trace to me"; "I worry about prints"; "judging from today I'd say grandpas gun does just fine idk."
- Robinson on the bullet engravings: "Remember how I was engraving bullets? The f***in messages are mostly a big meme, if I see 'notices bulge uwu' on fox new I might have a stroke."
- Robinson's closing instructions: **"Delete this exchange"**; don't talk to media; don't take interviews; "If police ask questions, ask for a lawyer and stay silent"; "I'm going to turn myself in willingly"; "You are all I worry about love... I'm much more worried about you." He also texted contemplating suicide: "I'm sorry, but I think this might be it... I have no intention of being drug through the courts in front of the country" (unsealed warrants, April 2026).

**Twiggs' response to the deletion instruction is the pivotal fact of his role: he did not delete the messages.** The preserved thread became the backbone of the charging document, and Robinson's instructions to Twiggs are the factual basis of the two **witness tampering** counts (The Advocate; PBS; The Hill).

### 3.4 The Discord thread

Discord's official statement (Sept. 2025) clarified an important early misreport: messages initially described as Robinson "planning" the attack on Discord were in fact **communications between the suspect's roommate [Twiggs] and a friend after the shooting, where the roommate was recounting the contents of a note the suspect had left elsewhere** (NBC live blog, Sept. 16–17, 2025). Separately, Robinson himself posted "it was me at UVU yesterday" to a Discord group about an hour before surrendering, and Twiggs showed investigators Discord messages attributed to Robinson discussing the rifle and engraved bullets (CNN, Sept. 29, 2025; AP/The Hill, July 9, 2026). On Day 4 (July 9), the two accounts of how this material entered the courtroom are reconcilable rather than contradictory: AP reports Discord messages were **read aloud** in court (Robinson's mother cried as they were read), while the KUTV liveblog reports the underlying **exhibits were not shown** on the broadcast and portions were redacted — i.e., read into the record through testimony, withheld as displayed exhibits. This substantially resolves the earlier AP-vs.-Washington Times display question tracked in this project's discrepancy register, subject to transcript confirmation.

### 3.5 September 11: the in-person encounter

From Twiggs' recorded interview as played July 9:

- Twiggs saw Robinson in their St. George apartment — over 200 miles from Orem — the day after the shooting. "I just asked him in person if what he said was true the night before, and he said it was, **started crying a little bit and said he wishes he hadn't done it**" (Fox News liveblog; AP).
- Robinson was pacing, "acting erratically," "doing stuff, I think just to keep himself busy or distracted or something" (CBS, July 9, 2026).
- Robinson said he planned to **talk to his parents or turn himself in**, and left the apartment. Twiggs said he then left too, because he "didn't want to be there regardless of what went down" (CBS; local12 liveblog).
- **Date discrepancy flag:** CBS rendered this encounter as "Sept. 12, the day after the shooting" — internally inconsistent (the day after Sept. 10 is Sept. 11). Robinson surrendered at the Washington County Sheriff's Office around 9–10 p.m. on **Sept. 11** (NPR quoting SBI Agent Brian Davis; CNN). The weight of the record places the tearful encounter on Sept. 11, before the evening surrender.

### 3.6 What Twiggs did *not* do — correcting the early record

- **Twiggs did not turn Robinson in.** The surrender chain, per charging documents: Robinson's parents recognized him in released surveillance images → his father confronted him by phone (Robinson "implied that he planned to take his own life") → parents convinced him to meet at their home, where he implied he was the shooter and said Kirk "spreads too much hate" → a family friend, a retired deputy sheriff, persuaded him to surrender (CNN; CBS; CNBC). The Daily Mail's early claim that Twiggs "tipped off police" is **contradicted by the official record**.
- A relative told Fox News Digital that, notwithstanding later descriptions of Twiggs as cooperative, **authorities initially had to locate him and bring him in for questioning** (Fox News, Jan. 15, 2026 — tier 3, single family source; not contradicted but not officially confirmed).

### 3.7 September 12: first law enforcement interview

Twiggs spoke to authorities on **Sept. 12, 2025** — per AP, two days after the shooting and the day after Robinson's late-evening surrender (AP/PBS, July 2026). *(Booking-time reporting for Robinson conflicts across sources; this document deliberately does not anchor Twiggs' first interview to the booking.)* Per SBI Agent Brian Davis's July 8 testimony, Twiggs was interviewed twice in total, **provided a DNA buccal swab**, and received limited-use immunity from state and federal prosecutors before cooperating (NewsNation; ABC4). He handed over his phone contents: the text thread, the photo of the note, and Discord material. Gov. Cox and Bongino publicly credited this material within days as central to establishing the case.

## 4. Legal Status: Immunity, Non-Suspect, Uncharged

- **Immunity type:** "**Use immunity**" (also reported as "limited-use immunity") — his statements cannot be used against him in a potential prosecution; it is **not** transactional immunity and does not categorically protect him from prosecution (ABC4, July 8, 2026; Daily Wire). Granted by both **state and federal** prosecutors (NewsNation).
- Before the second interview, prosecutors **warned Twiggs that knowingly making false statements could constitute a Class A misdemeanor** (Davis testimony, July 8, 2026 — NewsNation).
- **Twiggs has never been arrested or charged.** Authorities have consistently said they do not believe he participated in planning or executing the shooting (New York Times via Daily Wire, July 7, 2026; Cox: the partner "had no idea that this was happening").
- Utah DPS Commissioner Beau Mason (Sept. 15, 2025): authorities believed Robinson acted alone but were investigating whether anyone had foreknowledge. No subsequent official finding has implicated Twiggs.

**Analytical note:** The immunity grant is standard prosecutorial practice for securing testimony from a witness whose proximity to evidence creates theoretical exposure (e.g., obstruction theories). The defense explicitly identified the immunity agreements and "potential motives to cooperate" as a target for cross-examination (LAmag; Rev.com transcript) — a legitimate impeachment avenue that remains unexercised because Twiggs has not yet faced cross-examination.

## 5. Twiggs in the Physical Evidence (Preliminary Hearing, Days 2–3)

### 5.1 The DNA findings

Testimony from Utah DPS Sgt. Jennifer Faumuina and FBI forensic DNA examiner **Amanda Bakker** (July 7, 2026):

- Mixed male DNA from **two individuals — Robinson and Twiggs — was found on both** (a) the dark towel wrapped around the Mauser 98 rifle recovered in the woods, and (b) the **screwdriver recovered from the Losee Center rooftop** (the alleged firing position).
- Bakker, quoted directly: "When I calculated the statistics, Twiggs' DNA profile aligned with the **5%** contributor and Robinson's DNA profile aligned better with the **95%**" (towel); on the screwdriver the split was **Robinson ~89% / Twiggs ~11%** (Post Millennial video quote via Daily Wire; Fox News; Tippah/AP).
- Twiggs' profile came from his voluntarily provided buccal swab, used as an **elimination sample** (OANN; Fox News).
- **Media discrepancy flag:** One OANN article inverted the percentages (attributing 89%/95% to Twiggs). The direct Bakker quotation and the consensus of AP, Fox, NYT-derived, and Daily Wire reporting establish Robinson as the **majority** contributor and Twiggs as the **minority** contributor on both items.
- Bakker on cross-examination (defense attorney Michael Burt) made admissions properly labeled as such: FBI policy **prohibits describing DNA results as absolute identification**; her report identified Robinson only as a "possible contributor," not a confirmed source; testing "did not identify Robinson as the source" but found he "could not be excluded" (NewsNation, July 8, 2026). Burt argued "she can't match Mr. Robinson to the questioned samples" (CBS).

### 5.2 Competing interpretations

- **Prosecution:** cohabitation readily explains secondary transfer of a housemate's DNA onto household items (a towel, a tool); Twiggs' DNA presence is unremarkable and, if anything, corroborates that the items came from the shared apartment (OANN summarizing the state's position).
- **Defense:** Twiggs' DNA on two items tied to the crime — including one from the rooftop itself — is a wedge for reasonable doubt; DNA cannot establish *when* an item was touched or *who used it last* (OANN; consistent with the defense's broader third-party/reliability strategy).
- **What the record does not contain:** any official allegation that Twiggs was present at UVU, any explanation of the screwdriver's purpose, or any account of how/when either man's DNA was deposited. Note also the earlier fact from this project's prior research: Robinson's and Twiggs' DNA on the *screwdriver* coexists with the absence of on-roof firing residue evidence; the screwdriver remains an unexplained artifact.

## 6. The Fight Over His Testimony (May–July 2026)

### 6.1 Relocation and refusal of service

Twiggs **moved out of Utah to Texas**, where he reportedly lives with family (KUER; LAmag; court transcript). When the defense attempted to serve a subpoena, **Twiggs' counsel declined to accept service**, forcing the defense to seek court certification of an out-of-state subpoena under Utah Code §77-21-3 (Rev.com hearing transcript). This confirms Twiggs has retained his own attorney; the attorney has made no public statements.

### 6.2 Judge Graf's June 22, 2026 ruling

- The defense argued Twiggs is "a central, highly material witness whose testimony touches on nearly every charge and enhancement," that the Robinson–Twiggs communications underpin the death-penalty case, and that they must cross-examine him on reliability, credibility, his immunity agreements, and his state of mind (Rev.com transcript; ABC4).
- The prosecution did not intend to call Twiggs live, planning instead to use his **recorded April 20 interview** under Utah's preliminary-hearing hearsay allowance.
- **Graf quashed the subpoena** (without prejudice), holding: "The defendant has failed to identify specific anticipated testimony that is reasonably likely to negate probable cause, as distinguished from anticipated impeachment that would merely create competing credibility inferences" (ABC4; Fox News). He relied on *State v. Lopez* and the limited probable-cause function of preliminary hearings, and simultaneously denied the defense's motion to bar hearsay.
- Procedural chain, corrected against court reporting: the defense filed its application for an out-of-state witness certificate on **June 11, 2026**; Graf denied it June 22; the defense filed a **petition for permission to appeal on June 26** (ABC4/AOL) and, in filings reported around July 1, bundled the Twiggs/hearsay issue with its challenge to electronic media coverage in a petition to the **Utah Supreme Court**, arguing Twiggs "is, perhaps, the most critical witness in this entire case, a witness who, importantly, is not a victim," and that the combined rulings would deprive Robinson of compulsory process, due process, effective assistance, equal protection, and Eighth Amendment protections (KSL; Deseret News, July 1, 2026).
- **The Utah Supreme Court denied the petition on Thursday, July 2, 2026**: "The petition for permission to appeal from an interlocutory order is denied. The motion to stay is deemed moot" (KSL; East Idaho News). The hearing accordingly proceeded July 6–10 with Twiggs absent; cross-examination of Twiggs, if it occurs, awaits trial. *(Revision note: the original draft of this document inferred that the appeal "did not succeed in time"; verification established the stronger fact that the state's high court affirmatively declined review.)*

**Analytical note:** This is one of the more substantive structural criticisms available to the defense: the state's confession evidence enters the probable-cause record through a witness the defense has never been able to question. Graf's ruling is procedurally orthodox for Utah preliminary hearings, but the Confrontation Clause issue is preserved for trial, where Twiggs would have to testify live for his statements to be used in the same manner.

## 7. The Recorded Interviews and Their July 9, 2026 Airing

### 7.1 The two interviews

1. **September 12, 2025** — initial interview, day of Robinson's booking; under use immunity.
2. **April 20, 2026** — a **37-minute recorded interview at the Utah County Attorney's Office**, conducted by a prosecutor (reporting identifies prosecutor **Ryan McBride**; the court later ruled McBride's own statements in the recording were hearsay and directed focus onto Twiggs' answers). Given **under oath**, in lieu of live preliminary-hearing testimony, with the false-statement warning noted above (Front Page Detectives; ABC4; KUER; NewsNation). Twiggs appears in the video in a suit and tie (NBC).

### 7.2 The admissibility battle (Days 3–4)

- The defense fought public airing, arguing prosecutors would characterize the material as a confession and taint the jury pool, and proposed extensive redactions. The state said it was taken "essentially by surprise" by last-minute redaction demands, having disclosed the video to the defense in April (Post Millennial).
- Kirk family attorney **Jeffrey Neiman** argued for full, unredacted publication: "The Kirk family believes strongly that if the evidence is being admitted in this preliminary hearing, it should be made public for the world to see. No redactions" (Post Millennial; Daily Wire). Defense attorney Richard Novak countered that "the number one concern in this proceeding is Mr. Robinson's right to a fair trial" (ABC4; NPR).
- **Graf's resolution:** the interview was admitted, with redactions. **The publicly reported redaction figures conflict and cannot currently be reconciled:** NewsNation's Day 3 account says Graf "barred about three minutes of the interview from being played publicly"; The Post Millennial's Day 3 account says "around 17 minutes of the interview's audio" could not be played in the courtroom; Front Page Detectives' Day 4 account says redactions removed "a total of 16 minutes" from what it describes as a **37-minute recording** (FPD is the sole source for the total runtime). A possible — but inferential — reconciliation is that the ~3-minute figure refers to the narrower category of material barred from public broadcast (which ABC4 reported could still be shown to the court in a closed session), while the ~16–17-minute figures capture all material cut from courtroom play, including hearsay and relevance exclusions; additional redactions were also ordered on the morning of Day 4 (KUTV liveblog, 9:50 a.m.), so day-specific counts may measure different baselines. **Resolution path: the certified hearing transcript.** Portions were shown as audio-only or with blacked-out video; exhibits Twiggs was shown within the interview (the note, texts, Discord messages) were withheld from broadcast. Graf stated he would privately review the **full unredacted recording** in making the probable-cause determination, and that redactions at this stage do not control trial admissibility (OANN; The Federalist; KUTV liveblog).
- Graf denied Neiman's parallel demand that all admitted exhibits be displayed in real time to everyone in the courtroom: "Transparency is an important principle, but it must be maintained in a manner consistent with the constitutional rights and protections afforded to all parties" (The Federalist).

### 7.3 Substance of the interview as played (July 9)

Beyond the Sept. 10–11 narrative in §3, Twiggs stated:

- He **never heard Robinson talk about Charlie Kirk before the shooting** (CBS; NBC; local12).
- They **rarely discussed politics**; when they did, it centered on **President Trump and current events** — and Robinson **did not discuss gender identity or LGBTQ+ issues with him** (CBS; NewsNation).
- About **a month before the shooting**, Robinson asked to borrow a **Dremel tool to engrave bullets** for what Robinson described as **a planned hunting trip with his family** (NewsNation; NBC). This corroborates the engraved-casing evidence while supplying an innocent-sounding contemporaneous explanation Robinson allegedly gave.
- Shown surveillance photos of the suspect, Twiggs said he **"couldn't be 100% certain," but that they appeared to be Robinson** (local12 liveblog) — a qualified identification, properly noted as such.
- Courtroom observers reported Robinson **furrowed his brow and smirked** when his engraved-bullet messages with Twiggs were displayed; his mother cried as Discord messages were read (AP/The Hill).

## 9. Life Since September 2025

- Twiggs went almost entirely dark publicly — social accounts locked or deleted; no interviews; no statements. Speculative "where is Lance Twiggs" content (e.g., Distractify's "missing" framing) reflected his withdrawal, not any official missing-person status.
- Received FBI protection (with family members) due to threats; protection ended by January 2026 (Fox News — single law-enforcement source, tier 2/3 boundary).
- Relocated to **Texas** — established in court records (Graf's June 22 order and the defense's certificate application both reference compelling attendance "from Texas"; KUER; LAmag). ## 11. Key Open Questions

1. **Who burned the note, and when?** The original was recovered partially burned and forensically reconstructed; Twiggs says he photographed it and left it in Robinson's room. No public record assigns responsibility for the burning.
2. **The note's tense discrepancy** between the charging-document quotation and the displayed original.
3. **The screwdriver:** its purpose at the firing position, and the mechanism by which Twiggs' minority DNA arrived on it, remain unaddressed by any witness.
4. **The end and current character of the Robinson–Twiggs relationship** — undocumented beyond the "former partner" designation.
5. **Whether Twiggs will testify live at trial**, where confrontation rights attach fully; his credibility, immunity terms, and the Sept. 12 interview contents (never played publicly) would all be tested for the first time.
6. **The contents of the ~16–17 redacted minutes** of the April 20 interview, and the full Sept. 12 interview, which Judge Graf reviewed privately for the probable-cause decision. Graf did not rule immediately at the hearing's close; both sides will brief post-transcript, with oral arguments set for **September 1, 2026** (Christian Post, July 10, 2026).
