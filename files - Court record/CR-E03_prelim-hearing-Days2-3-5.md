## 3. DAY 5 — CAITLIN OLIVER, ATF DNA SECTION CHIEF (defense witness)

**Who she is.** ATF **DNA Section Chief** since January 2026; ~9 years at ATF; a **forensic biologist** in September 2025, when she did this work. Previously DNA analyst and DNA quality manager, Jefferson Parish Sheriff's Office, Louisiana. **She authored all three ATF DNA reports in this case**, including Exhibit 30 (the rifle) and the Dremel report. Assigned **2025-09-12**.

### 3.1 What the DNA cannot say — from the State's own analyst

Every item below is **her sworn agreement**, elicited by the defense:

- **Activity is unknowable.** "None of the testing that I performed could speak to activity or what activity led to the deposition of the DNA." Major/minor contributor labels "should not be interpreted in the context of… the nature, or the mechanisms, actions, or timing that led to the deposition."
- **Presence does not require contact.** "A DNA profile may be developed even when the contributor of that DNA never touched the item or area that was swabbed." Her own example: two people handle a pen; a swab cannot say who handled it last. On secondary transfer to a trigger: "It is possible, yes."
- **No source attribution.** Asked whether one could report "Mr. Robinson's DNA is found on this item," she agreed that is a source attribution and **"would not be scientifically defensible."** The DOJ ULTR for DNA forbids asserting that a likelihood ratio "of any magnitude provides an absolute identification or source attribution."
- **The number is a cap, not a measurement.** "We cap the statistic at one trillion. The actual number could be a lot higher. It could be quintillion, octillion." **This cuts toward the State** and is recorded as such: "at least one trillion" is a floor.
- **ATF uses no verbal scale** — five conclusions only (exclusion, limited support for exclusion, uninformative, limited support for inclusion, support for inclusion). "The likelihood ratio statistic, the number stands on its own."
- **All evidence samples were mixtures**, "in many cases… of sometimes four and five people." Some samples showed **degradation**.
- "The **true number of contributors** of DNA to an item **can never be known**." Agreed.

### 3.2 Consumption and elimination — two documented gaps

**Samples entirely consumed** (no independent retest possible): **1.4, 1.6, 1.9, 1.10, 1.12, 2.1, 3.1, 4.1, 5.1.** Among these are **the bolt (1.4), the barrel (1.6), the scope (1.9)** and **the cartridges from the firearm (3.1, 5.1)** — i.e. several of the swabs whose likelihood ratios CR-E02 §5.1 records. **Extracts retained** for 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.5, 1.7, 1.8, 1.13, 7.1, 8.1 (the trigger/trigger-guard sample, 1.3, is among those retained).

Permission to consume was obtained from the agent before work began — standard procedure asks the agent to reach out to an attorney if one is assigned. Burt drew out the **1996 NRC recommendation** that samples be split where possible, "a wrongfully accused person's best insurance against the possibility of being falsely incriminated." Oliver agreed the principle is longstanding and that her lab avoids consumption "where possible."

**Elimination samples were requested from the investigating agents and never received.** Oliver: "The type of testing we perform is quite sensitive. So, it is a standard practice that we request elimination samples from any of the investigators involved in order to potentially eliminate them, **in case they could have contaminated the evidence**." **Confidence: High** that this was testified. **This is a real, documented gap** and is recorded as one — without any suggestion that contamination in fact occurred.

### 3.3 Other people's DNA on the key items

Elimination samples were obtained from **Amber Robinson, Matthew Robinson and Lance Twiggs** as household associates. Oliver confirmed:

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| **1.4 — swabs of the rifle's BOLT** | "support for inclusion of **Matthew Robinson**" (Tyler's father) |
| **1.6 — swabs of the rifle's BARREL** | "support for inclusion of **Matthew Robinson**" |
| **Buttons of the Dremel tool** | "support for inclusion of **Lance Twiggs**" |
| Other areas of the Dremel | support for inclusion of Twiggs |

**Analyst layer.** These are **not** accusations and Oliver expressly framed them as expected: "It's reasonable to assume that anyone living in the household could have contributed to DNA on an object found in that household." **But they materially qualify CR-E02 §5.4b**, which recorded Robinson's DNA on the Dremel buttons at better than one in a trillion without noting that **Twiggs' DNA is there too**, or that the rifle's bolt and barrel carry his father's. The State's items are mixtures, and the mixtures have names in them. **CR-E02 §5.4b is corrected accordingly (§6).**

## 5. DAY 2 — AMANDA BAKKER, FBI DNA CASEWORK UNIT (defense witness) — OPENING ONLY

**Who she is.** Forensic examiner, **DNA Casework Unit, FBI Laboratory, Quantico**; 10+ years. Author of **State's Exhibit 31**, report dated **2025-09-15**.

- FBI first received evidence **2025-09-11**.
- The case ran at **Priority 1**: "we worked 24 hours a day, seven days a week until examinations were complete." Normal turnaround is 180–200 days. **Testing began while evidence was still being inventoried and delivered.**
- **Communication log, 2025-09-13**, read aloud verbatim: *"Spoke with FBI Special Agent Folger and **Utah SBI SA Dave Hull**. Gave technically reviewed results that **Robinson was included as a possible contributor to the DNA from the towel and the screwdriver**. We discussed the additional examinations conducted by DCU, and they both agreed that no additional immediate comparisons are needed at this time."*

**Analyst layer.** **The screwdriver was recovered from the Losee Center rooftop** (CR-E02 §2), and **the towel was wrapped around the rifle** in the wooded area (CR-E02 §4). Robinson being "included as a possible contributor" to DNA on both is, on this record, **the most direct evidentiary link yet recorded between him and the rooftop itself**. **Confidence: High** that Bakker so testified and so logged it on 2025-09-13. **All of §3.1's limitations apply with equal force** — "included as a possible contributor" is not source attribution, and says nothing about how or when the DNA arrived. **Exhibit 31 has not been read by this project**, and **Bakker's testimony past her opening is unread**, so the likelihood ratios behind these inclusions are **unknown**.

## 6. CORRECTION TO CR-E02 §5.4b

CR-E02 §5.4b (v1.2) recorded the Dremel chain — the toolmark identification and Robinson's DNA on the buttons at better than one in a trillion — and called it "the strongest evidentiary chain in anything this project has read." **That stands as to the toolmark identification, which is unaffected.** But the DNA leg must now carry two qualifications from Oliver's testimony: **Lance Twiggs is also supported for inclusion on the Dremel's buttons and elsewhere on the tool**, and **the rifle's bolt and barrel support inclusion of Matthew Robinson.** Neither is surprising in a shared household, and neither is exculpatory in itself — **but a chain described without them overstates.** Recorded here and cross-referenced there.
