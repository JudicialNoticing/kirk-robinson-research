## 3. DAY 5 — CAITLIN OLIVER, ATF DNA SECTION CHIEF (defense witness)

**Who she is.** ATF **DNA Section Chief** since January 2026; ~9 years at ATF; a **forensic biologist** in September 2025, when she did this work. Previously DNA analyst and DNA quality manager, Jefferson Parish Sheriff's Office, Louisiana. **She authored all three ATF DNA reports in this case**, including Exhibit 30 (the rifle) and the Dremel report. Assigned **2025-09-12**.

### 3.1 What the DNA cannot say — from the State's own analyst

Every item below is **her sworn agreement**, elicited by the defense:

- **Activity is unknowable.** "None of the testing that I performed could speak to activity or what activity led to the deposition of the DNA." Major/minor contributor labels "should not be interpreted in the context of… the nature, or the mechanisms, actions, or timing that led to the deposition."
- **Presence does not require contact.** "A DNA profile may be developed even when the contributor of that DNA never touched the item or area that was swabbed." Her own example: two people handle a pen; a swab cannot say who handled it last. On secondary transfer to a trigger: "It is possible, yes."
- **No source attribution.** Asked whether one could report "Mr. Robinson's DNA is found on this item," she agreed that is a source attribution and **"would not be scientifically defensible."** The DOJ ULTR for DNA forbids asserting that a likelihood ratio "of any magnitude provides an absolute identification or source attribution."
- **The number is a cap, not a measurement.** "We cap the statistic at one trillion. The actual number could be a lot higher. It could be quintillion, octillion." **This cuts toward the State** and is recorded as such: "at least one trillion" is a floor. ⚠ **Scoped 2026-07-28 (§5A.1):** the cap is **ATF's own reporting convention** — her word is "**we** cap" — and applies to the ATF reports (Exhibits 30, 33). **The FBI does not apply it**: Exhibit 31's figures for the towel and screwdriver are reported **uncapped**, at 1.7 octillion and 30 quintillion. Numbers from the two labs are not comparable without naming the lab.
- **ATF uses no verbal scale** — five conclusions only (exclusion, limited support for exclusion, uninformative, limited support for inclusion, support for inclusion). "The likelihood ratio statistic, the number stands on its own."
- **All evidence samples were mixtures**, "in many cases… of sometimes four and five people." Some samples showed **degradation**.
- "The **true number of contributors** of DNA to an item **can never be known**." Agreed.

### 3.2 Consumption and elimination — two documented gaps

**Samples entirely consumed** (no independent retest possible): **1.4, 1.6, 1.9, 1.10, 1.12, 2.1, 3.1, 4.1, 5.1.** Among these are **the bolt (1.4), the barrel (1.6), the scope (1.9)** and **the cartridges from the firearm (3.1, 5.1)** — i.e. several of the swabs whose likelihood ratios CR-E02 §5.1 records. **Extracts retained** for 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, 1.5, 1.7, 1.8, 1.13, 7.1, 8.1 (the trigger/trigger-guard sample, 1.3, is among those retained).

Permission to consume was obtained from the agent before work began — standard procedure asks the agent to reach out to an attorney if one is assigned. Burt drew out the **1996 NRC recommendation** that samples be split where possible, "a wrongfully accused person's best insurance against the possibility of being falsely incriminated." Oliver agreed the principle is longstanding and that her lab avoids consumption "where possible."

**Elimination samples were requested from the investigating agents and never received.** Oliver: "The type of testing we perform is quite sensitive. So, it is a standard practice that we request elimination samples from any of the investigators involved in order to potentially eliminate them, **in case they could have contaminated the evidence**." **Confidence: High** that this was testified. **This is a real, documented gap** and is recorded as one — without any suggestion that contamination in fact occurred.
