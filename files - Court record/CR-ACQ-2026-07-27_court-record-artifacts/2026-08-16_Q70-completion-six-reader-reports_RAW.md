## A. Findings — RECORDING layer (what the filings say, attributed)

**A1. What dkt 250 asks for.** Defence asks the court to find that a *named-but-redacted* prosecutor "representing the State of Utah for the Utah County Attorney's Office ('UCAO')" has a concurrent conflict of interest based on "personal connections to a direct witness/victim"; to find (1) UCAO "did not employ any screening protocol" on learning of it and (2) the information was "shared amongst the assigned prosecutorial team, including the County Attorney"; and to disqualify **the entire UCAO**. Attributed to defence, dkt 250 p.2 of 14.

**A2. Target: the prosecutor's office only. No judge is targeted.** Nothing in any of the three filings seeks disqualification or recusal of Judge Tony F. Graf, Jr., or any judge. Graf appears only in the caption line of all three. (See D1.)

**A3. The asserted conflict — factual basis, attributed, not adopted.** Per dkt 250 pp.4–5: a student in attendance at the 2025-09-10 Kirk event was a close family member (relationship term redacted) of the redacted prosecutor. At the time of the shooting that prosecutor was with County Attorney Jeffrey Gray at a presentation in Davis County; he received texts from the family member and "shared those with Mr. Gray in real time," and also phoned another UCAO attorney "almost immediately." The filing reproduces the text exchange with timestamps 12:25, 12:26, 12:30, 12:33, 12:38 PM. Defence states the prosecutor "holds a supervisory role at the UCAO as the [redacted] under Mr. Gray," "remains on and is still the [redacted] of the prosecution team," has said the family member "will not be a witness for the State," and does not believe a conflict exists.

**A4. Disclosure chronology asserted in dkt 250.** 09-29-2025: prosecutor contacts UCAO investigator C. Christensen for help measuring the family member's distance from Kirk (Ex. H). 09-30-2025: prosecutor and Christensen go to UVU and tape-measure ≈**85 feet** from Kirk's seated position to where the family member's backpack appears in photographs. 10-01-2025: prosecutor discloses by email to defence counsel. 10-09-2025: defence speaks with the prosecutor and Christopher Ballard; "additional information concerning communications within the UCAO was provided." (Note the ordering: the measurement preceded the disclosure by one day.)

**A5. Death-notice timing argument.** Defence asserts the notice of intent to seek death (Dkt No. 10) was filed 2025-09-16 concurrently with the Information, "[l]ess than one week after" the prosecutor's real-time communications reached the County Attorney, though not required until 60 days after arraignment; defence calls this "[t]he rush to seek death" evidencing "strong emotional reactions." dkt 250 p.9. Attributed argument, not established fact.

**A6. Legal theory.** Utah R. Prof'l Cond. 1.7(a)(2) concurrent conflict; imputation to the whole office under *State v. McClellan*, 2009 UT 50 (which defence says disqualified this same office, the UCAO, where there was "no evidence of any attempt" to screen); *State v. Balfour*, 2008 UT App 410; *People v. Connor*, 666 P.2d 5 (Cal. 1983); *State v. Tate*, 925 S.W.2d 548 (Tenn. 1995). Due process: U.S. Const. amends. V, XIV; Utah Const. art. I §7; *Marshall v. Jerrico*; *Ganger v. Peyton*. Eighth Amendment heightened-reliability framing throughout.

**A7. dkt 293 — what it asks.** Not a merits reply. It gives notice that defence will call witnesses at the **2026-01-16** hearing, asks to supplement briefing after an evidentiary hearing, and asserts the State's opposition rests on unsworn assertions — specifically that ¶¶11–14 of the opposition (at pp.4–5) and the death-decision discussion (at p.14) are "not supported by affidavit or sworn declaration." Footnote 1: subpoenas served, "**All four witnesses have accepted service** as of this filing."

**A8. dkt 293 footnote 2 — the seed of the AG request.** Defence argues UCAO "has a vested interest in the outcome of the motion" and that "the scope of the disclosures and litigation of this motion should have been assigned to another state agency, such as the Attorney General." dkt 293 pp.3–4 n.2. This footnote is what dkt 348 later builds on.

**A9. dkt 348 — what it actually asks (critical).** It does **not** seek to disqualify the Utah Attorney General. It asks the court to **refer the pending disqualification motion to the AG** under Utah Code §17-68-304 — i.e. to make the statutory finding that the County Attorney "is unable to satisfactorily and adequately perform the duties of prosecuting a criminal case" and to "recommend that [he] seek additional legal assistance," which triggers the AG's duty to assist. Secondary ask: **adjourn the evidentiary hearing and postpone all further proceedings on the motion** until the AG acts. Authorities: Utah Code §67-5-1(1)(f) (AG supervisory power over county attorneys); *State v. Jimenez*, 588 P.2d 707 (Utah 1978); *State v. Robertson*, 924 P.2d 889 (Utah 1996).

**A10. dkt 348 asserts a SECOND, newly-emerged conflict — Gray's own.** Defence states that during his sworn testimony on 2026-01-16, Gray revealed that "the children of two members of his ward were also present at Mr. Kirk's rally on September 10, 2025, and that one of them was 'shook up'" (citing Dkt. 332 at 93); that Gray testified he was unaware of this personal relationship until after the Information was filed (id. at 92); and that Gray "kept it secret until he was asked about it while testifying under oath." Footnote 3: defence counsel were not advised of this before the 01-16 testimony. Defence also states Gray "has acknowledged that he did not" report the conflict scenario to the AG's office. **All attributed to defence's characterisation of testimony this project has not read.**

**A11. dkt 348 proposes a legal standard.** For triggering §17-68-304 in a capital case, defence proposes "no more than a **non-frivolous factual predicate**" that a disqualifying conflict exists, citing *Ford v. Wainwright*, 477 U.S. 399, 411 (1986). It states the court asked the parties at the 01-16 hearing what standard to apply and that "[t]he Utah Code does not answer this question, nor is there any clear case law."

**A12. dkt 348 records a rebuke and a media complaint.** Defence concedes the court "expressed concern" that the 01-16 verbal referral request "took the Court and opposing counsel by surprise," and counsel "take full responsibility." It characterises Gray's response — that the request is "another stalling tactic to delay these proceedings" (Dkt. 332 at 21) — as "disingenuous," and complains of "increasingly prejudicial media coverage," citing Sen. Mike Lee's post-hearing call for public execution (NY Post, 2026-01-21).

## A. Findings — RECORDING layer (what the State says, attributed)

**The legal standard the State advances**

- **A1.** The State accepts the defence's general principles (URPC 1.7; URPC 3.8 cmt. 1; *Marshall v. Jerrico*; *State v. - **A2.** The operative test the State says the defence must meet: a "significant risk" that representation of the State "will be materially limited … by a personal interest of the lawyer," URPC 1.7(a)(2) — and the burden "rests with Defendant." — dkt 289 pp.6, 9.
- **A3.** The State imports URPC 3.7 (lawyer-as-witness) as an analytic aid: disqualification is not required where the potential testimony is "either unnecessary or relates to an uncontested issue." — dkt 289 p.7.
- **A4.** On imputation: "[w]hen disqualification is appropriate, it is usually sufficient to disqualify the particular attorney with a conflict rather than the entire office," *Balfour* ¶ 34, resting on *United States v. - **A6.** New authority the State adds in dkt 366: *People v. Cope*, 50 P.3d 513 (Kan. App. 2002); *State v. Pennington*, 365 S.E.2d 803 (W. Va. 1987) — the question is "whether the prosecutor's interest is public or personal." — dkt 366 pp.9–10.

## 2. What the entered order actually requires (the operative terms)

From the 2025-11-04 stipulated order, PDF pp. 3–4:

**¶1 — Set-aside.** The 2025-10-20 order entitled "Order Granting Motion to Preserve Evidence" (Dkt. #107) is SET ASIDE.

**¶2 — Who and what.** Bound: **the Utah County Attorney's Office, its agents, and any law enforcement agencies involved in the investigation of this case.** Duty: preserve **"all evidence in their custody or under their control, involved in the investigation of this case."**
- **No category list.** No mention of biological, DNA, digital, ballistic, or documentary evidence. It is a single undifferentiated class.
- **No materiality qualifier.** The State's proposed "all **material** evidence" did **not** survive.
- **The order does not reach anyone else** — no federal agency is named or described, and no agency outside "involved in the investigation of this case" is bound.

**¶3 — Release/disposal exit.** UCAO may release or dispose of an item if (a) it notifies defence counsel of intent, **and** (b) defence counsel does not object **within 14 days** of receiving notice, or agrees.

**¶4 — Pre-testing notice (the operative provision for the DNA question).** UCAO and any involved law enforcement agency **must notify defence counsel prior to any potentially destructive testing and/or examination**; the same 14-day non-objection/agreement window then permits it to proceed. And the order **defines the trigger**:

> "Testing and/or examination is potentially destructive only when it is likely to entirely consume a piece of evidence, or an extract from that piece of evidence, or is likely to change the character of a piece of evidence."

**Duration:** none stated. No sunset, no retention period, no reference to the Utah evidence-retention schedules (§§ 77-11c-301, -401(2)) that both parties briefed. The order is silent on how long it runs.

**Note the word "only."** The definition is *restrictive* — it narrows the notice trigger rather than illustrating it. Testing that consumes part of a sample but is not "likely to entirely consume" the piece or the extract, and does not change the item's character, falls outside ¶4.
