**Headline results — four.**

1. **The strongest form of the no-casing finding is now on the record, and it is a *purposive* negative.** The first officer onto the roof, **Officer Christopher Bagley**, searched **specifically for a spent shell casing** — at the prone position, over the edge, "all over the place" — **and found none**, and he explained why he knew to look. A second witness, the scene commander, independently said no bullets or casings were found on the roof or immediately around the building. **This is not a gap in the record; it is a search that returned nothing.** (§6)
2. **Nothing in the five banked hearing days establishes a discharge *at that location*.** Full-text searches of all five transcripts return **zero** occurrences of *gunshot residue / GSR / primer residue*, **zero** of *trajectory*, and **zero** of *reconstruct*. The rooftop case is **positional and circumstantial** — a gravel impression, a sight line, video of a prone figure, a screwdriver, a shoe imprint — and the record contains **no forensic test of the roof surface for a firing event at all.** This is a claim about these transcripts, not about the world (§6.2).
3. **The sight line was conceded and immediately qualified in the same breath.** Asked whether there is "a clear line of sight between the tent and the top of the Losee Center," Bagley answered "Yes, there is" — and on the **very next question** agreed some of it is **obstructed** by the Sorensen Center, confining the tent sight line to **"the far south side of the Losee building."** That is **the same portion of the roof where he independently found the gravel impression.** The coherence cuts for the State; the fact that it is a **lay eyeball estimate from photographs**, by a witness who expressly **was not a sniper**, cuts the other way. Both are logged (§5).
4. **The perch is an investigative *belief*; the distances are measurements; and the measurements' provenance is unestablished.** Sgt. Faumuina described "**what we believe was the shooter's perch**," identified at night by **oblique lighting** showing gravel disturbance, and gave **approximately 415 ft slope / 410 ft horizontal / 68 ft vertical** from perch to tent. The three figures are one internally consistent survey (√(410²+68²) ≈ 415.6). **But no witness said who took those measurements, when, or by what method** — a targeted search of Day 4 returns no such testimony. **Grade the perch identification and the geometry separately, always** (§4.3).

## 4. THE PERCH — recording layer

### 4.1 What the first officer saw, and how he qualified his own exhibit

Bagley reached the rooftop at **12:44** (his own recollection; `day1.txt` 01:43:05). He **hopped the guardrail** and, on the **southwest** side of the roof, found **a disturbance in the gravel**.

**His characterisation, verbatim** (`day1.txt` 02:26:12, stepping down to the monitor to explain **State's Exhibit 4**, a nighttime photograph):

> "To me, when I got up there and I could see this disturbance of gravel, to me, **it looks like a sniper pad, a person that has been laying in a prone position and you've got markings of elbows, knees, and feet** to where somebody was in the line of sight of where Charlie's tent was."

⚠ **His own qualification of the photographic evidence is preserved, because it is the single most important caveat on any rooftop image** (`day1.txt` 02:18:42–02:19:04, on **State's Exhibit 3.2**, the daytime view):

> **Q:** "And does this photograph do it justice?" — **A:** "**No.**"
> **Q:** "And how so?" — **A:** "You can't really see the impression I saw the time of when I was up there on that day. **I saw a distinct impression in the gravel and this does not show that.**"
> **Q:** "So you could see more with the naked eye?" — **A:** "Yes."

He located it on **State's Exhibit 2** as "the darker gray part… **the south side** of the Losee building… **on the southwest side of the roof**" (02:26:55). He **did not walk through or touch** the disturbance (02:41:17–02:41:25).

> **Analyst layer — recording and analyst layers held apart.** "**Sniper pad**" is **the witness's own word**, offered as an impression ("to me… to me"), by a witness who **expressly was not a sniper**. It is recorded with fidelity **and is adopted by nobody.** ⚠ **Any use of the rooftop photographs anywhere in this library must carry his "this does not show that."** The exhibits show *less* than the witness says he saw — which is a caution running **against** the images as proof of the impression, and simultaneously a fact **for** the State's account of what was on the roof. **Confidence: High** as to what he said (both passages read verbatim 2026-08-01). **What would change it:** the exhibits themselves, or any contemporaneous scene photograph or measurement of the impression.

### 4.2 How the crime lab identified and processed it

**Sgt. Jennifer Faumuina** (Utah DPS/SBI) was assigned on **2025-09-10 at approximately 1:30 p.m.** and identified **four scenes**: the amphitheatre; the Fulton Library (two bus stops); a wooded area; and **the Losee Center building and the ground below**. The rooftop was processed by the **Utah Bureau of Forensic Services** — the state crime lab (CR-E02 §2).

**Her account of the perch, verbatim** (`day4.txt` 04:35:59, verified directly 2026-08-01):

> "One item of evidence that was found was a screwdriver. Another one would've been **what we believe was the shooter's perch**. That was determined based… or that was **easy to see at night using oblique lighting**. We could see **disturbances in the gravel on the rear rooftop**. And then on the **northeast edge**, our team also found some **swipes on the ledge** of that rear rooftop, that was also collected."

Also on the rooftop: **shoe impressions** (04:38:28), collected along with the latent prints by those who processed the scene.

### 6.4 What the absence of a roof casing does and does not mean

**It is consistent with the State's own theory of the weapon.** Faumuina testified to the mechanics: the rifle is **bolt action** — the user must manually work the bolt to load, lock and extract, unlike a semi-automatic that extracts and re-feeds on each trigger pull; **a spent case therefore remains in the chamber unless manually extracted.** And the fired case in evidence, **State's Exhibit 19, is "the cartridge case that was recovered from the gun," from the chamber** (`day4.txt` 04:55:45; CR-E02 §4). **On that account, no ejected casing would be expected on the roof at all.**

**But the same fact cuts the other way for the ballistic chain**, and this is the point the superseded file did not carry: **a fired case found *inside* a rifle is expected to identify to that rifle.** The ATF's `source identification` therefore links the case to the **weapon**, not the weapon to the **shooting**; and the only projectile evidence from the body — the **6A bullet jacket fragment** — was **inconclusive**. **Nothing recovered at the perch bears on it, because nothing was recovered at the perch.**

> **Analyst layer — FINDING 4.** **The absence of a casing on the roof is evidentially close to neutral**, and both halves must travel together. **It is not a hole in the State's case** (bolt-action mechanics explain it, and the chamber case is in evidence) **and it is not corroboration either** (a negative consistent with a theory does not confirm the theory; it merely fails to refute it). **What it does do is sharpen D-48**: the ATF firearms examination connects a case from the rifle's own chamber to the rifle, and **on this record the ATF firearms examination does not link the rifle to the shooting.** **Confidence: Medium-high** — the mechanics and the chamber recovery are Tier 1 testimony; **the ATF report itself has been read directly** (CR-ACQ §3.1), but the identity mapping of exhibit numbers across the two numbering systems is a well-supported **inference**, graded there and flagged as under challenge at Q-42 (human-gated). **The direction of D-48 is unaffected either way.**
