PROVO, Utah (KUTV) — The final day of the preliminary hearing for the man accused of killing Charlie Kirk began on Friday morning, with arguments over media coverage and DNA analysis.

Tyler Robinson, the man accused of killing political activist Charlie Kirk, appeared in court for the hearing as prosecutors worked to prove probable cause.

Multiple investigators and experts testified on the stand throughout the five-day hearing, discussing DNA evidence, interviews with Robinson's romantic partner and ballistic reports.

The court adjourned on Friday and will return on Sept. 1 for Judge Tony Graf's ruling on whether the state presented enough evidence for the case to go to trial. We will likely also hear Robinson's plea at that time.

KUTV posted live updates on the hearing. Follow along below.

12:00 p.m. - The preliminary hearing adjourned. The court will return on Sept. 1 to hear Judge Graf's determination on whether the case should go to trial.

11:43 a.m. - Tyler Robinson follows his attorney's advice and chooses not to testify.

11:40 a.m. - When recross-examined by the defense, Oliver confirmed that there was DNA degradation in the sample of the trigger and trigger guard, and that there was DNA from multiple people on the swab. She clarified that's why they determine who is the major contributor, meaning whose DNA appears the most.

11:35 a.m. - The state reviewed the DNA report's conclusion with the witness, which was that it was at least one trillion times more likely to have originated from Robinson as the major contributor than from multiple unrelated, unknown individuals.

11:27 a.m. - The defense concludes their questioning of Oliver, and the state begins to cross-examine her.

11:07 a.m. - Oliver said they found DNA of people in the house, including Robinson's dad and Lance Twiggs, on the items they tested. She said that's not unexpected, as anyone living in the household could have their DNA on the items whether they touched it or not.

11:00 a.m. - The defense questioned Oliver about DNA reports. She said that DNA evidence does not show who handled the item last, meaning it could show multiple people's DNA. Oliver said it's also possible that someone's DNA could be on an item they never touched, for example, if they shook hands with someone who then touched the item.

Oliver said DNA is quite stable, but does degrade over time or with environmental factors. She said there was DNA degradation on some of the items that they tested.

10:33 a.m. - Defense called their second witness, forensics expert Caitlin Oliver, to the stand. Oliver is a forensic biologist with the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives.

10:23 a.m. - Judge Graf ruled that the court order was violated, but denied the defense's requests to remove cameras from this hearing and all future proceedings. Instead, he provided the remedy that cameras would not be allowed to capture any evidence displayed in court during today's hearing.

Judge Graf said he will continue to consider media's involvement on a hearing-by-hearing basis.

10:14 a.m. - The state proposed a different remedy of having the media delay the livestream for 30 seconds in future proceedings.

The attorney for the media said they have been cooperative with the court's requested changes throughout the proceedings. He also noted that the camera operator moved the camera before anyone in the court noticed the mistake.

10:10 a.m. - The court returned from a brief recess after Judge Graf finished reviewing the livestream. The defense proposed a remedy to remove electronic media coverage in all future proceedings. They gave a "less desirable" alternative remedy, asking for the court to remove media for today's hearing and for the hearing on Sept. 1.

9:23 a.m. - The defense team raised a concern about an instance on Thursday in which the media briefly livestreamed a piece of evidence that had been court-ordered not to be shown. The state had placed the evidence on the large monitor by mistake. When the camera operator realized it was a new exhibit, they moved the camera away within three and a half seconds.

The defense team said that image is now out in the public, and it's a permanent consequence, as they cannot remove it from the internet.

Judge Graf said this instance was on his mind a lot after the hearing, and he wants to address it now.

The state recognized the mistake, but noted that the defense did not make an objection after it occurred. The media attorney argued that the camera operator did not violate the order as the image was shown on the screen without warning, and they reacted quickly to move the camera.

9:15 a.m. - Court returns with Judge Tony Graf reviewing decorum orders.
