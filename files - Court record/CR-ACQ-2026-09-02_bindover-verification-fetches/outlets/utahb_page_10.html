Tyler Robinson to stand trial for allegedly killing Charlie Kirk, judge confirms

Sorina Trauntvein and Amelia Hobson
1 day ago

PROVO, Utah (ABC4) — Less than two weeks ahead of the one-year anniversary of Charlie Kirk’s assassination, the man accused of his murder appeared in court for final arguments and a decision on his preliminary hearing.

23-year-old Tyler Robinson has been charged with aggravated murder, a capital first-degree felony, alongside five additional felonies and a misdemeanor for the alleged assassination of Charlie Kirk.


At the end of Tuesday’s arguments, Judge Tony Graf ruled that Tyler Robinson will be going to trial for allegedly killing Charlie Kirk, binding over the case on all counts. Following the court’s decision, Robinson pleaded not guilty.

The court also set an in-person pretrial conference for October 23 at 1 p.m. The victim representative, on behalf of Erika Kirk, asked that a trial date be set at that conference if possible.

If convicted, Robinson could face the death penalty.

Graf stated that he reviewed all written statements, evidence presented throughout Robinson’s preliminary hearing and evidentiary hearing, and the arguments from the prosecution and from the defense in making his decision.

The initial phase of Robinson’s preliminary hearing, a five-day run in court from July 6 to 10, featured testimony from witnesses, experts, and new evidence, including a recorded testimony from Robinson’s former romantic partner and roommate, images of text messages between them, surveillance footage from UVU, and new photographs of the rifle and other evidence connected to the shooting.

In his ruling, Graf stated that the court would not assign a quantitative analysis to the possibility of the bullet ricocheting or causing shrapnel. However, he said the prosecution did show evidence to support a reasonable inference that Robinson’s alleged actions could have injured another individual and a reasonable inference that Robinson would have been aware of this.

While Graf did not and cannot make a finding of facts on these reasonable inferences, he says the prosecution’s argument met the preliminary hearing’s standards for bind over on the count of aggravated murder.

Judge Graf then went over each of the remaining counts, issuing rulings on each one and determining each court can be bound over for trial.

Arguments on media coverage

The final day of the preliminary hearing kicked off on Sept. 1 at 10 a.m. in a closed session, during which the judge and attorneys discussed a motion from the defense to close the hearing to the media.

Arguments continued after the hearing was reopened, with the defense saying that they wanted to remove part of the support for their argument because they did not want it in the public eye. The prosecution argued that the defense had already made the argument, and they couldn’t “put the toothpaste back in the tube.”

Judge Tony F. Graf, Jr. ruled that the defense could not withdraw that support for transparency, and further ruled that cameras would be allowed in the courtroom during the final day of the preliminary hearing.

The prosecution and defense will each receive up to two hours for arguments. The prosecution stated that it expects to take less than an hour. Afterward, the court will be in recess for up to an hour while the judge reviews the arguments. He will then issue a ruling from the bench.

Before the court went on lunch break, the prosecution discussed pieces of evidence that were previously not admitted into court during the preliminary hearing and requested that they be allowed during arguments.

Prosecution argues for bindover

Following the recess, Deputy Utah County Attorney Ryan McBride began his arguments by stating that Robinson was responsible for the murder of Charlie Kirk using a high-powered rifle that placed many others in danger, all because he disagreed with him politically.

McBride pointed to surveillance footage as the first piece of evidence, retracing Robinson’s path across campus in the hours before the deadly shooting. He said that following two scouting missions, Robinson returned to campus “dressed to kill,” with a rifle stuffed down his pant leg.

The prosecution alleges that Robinson looked across the crowd of 3,000 people from a prone position on the Losee Center rooftop and fatally shot Kirk at 12:28 p.m.

He then explained how Robinson fled the scene, stashed the firearm, and waited in a nearby neighborhood before leaving the area. A police officer collected a partial license plate from Robinson’s vehicle as he went through campus that night.

McBride pointed to evidence that was discussed during the preliminary hearing, where law enforcement witnesses explained how the gun was connected to Robinson and the shooting, as well as a message between Robinson and his roommate/former romantic partner Lance Twiggs saying, “Judging from today, I’d say grandpa’s gun does just fine.”

“Make no mistake, your honor, this is the firearm that killed Charlie Kirk,” McBride stated.

Surveillance footage played, forensic examiner testifies during Tyler Robinson’s preliminary hearing

The prosecution also explained that Robinson had confessed to the murder, not only to law enforcement, but to friends in a Discord chat, telling them to look at the surveillance footage when they questioned him.

McBride explained that Kirk was an outspoken critic of homosexuality and transgender individuals, and Robinson was in a relationship with Twiggs, who was considering transitioning genders and went by a female name. This would give Robinson the motive.

The Deputy County Attorney argued for the aggravating circumstances, noting that any reasonable person would know that a public assassination poses a great risk of injuring or even killing someone else nearby.

When Robinson looked out over the crowd, he saw the large crowd and still decided to fire, and had additional bullets on hand. If he had missed hitting Kirk, the prosecution argued he was prepared to keep firing.

Testimony of Tyler Robinson’s former romantic partner played, photos of rifle and engraved bullets shown

McBride explained the charges Robinson is facing: aggravated murder, a capital first-degree felony; discharge of a firearm causing serious bodily injury, a first-degree felony; two counts of obstruction of justice, a second-degree felony; two counts of tampering with a witness, a third-degree felony; and committing a violent offense in the presence of a child, a Class A misdemeanor.

“Your Honor, in summary, on September 10, 2025, the defendant Tyler James Robinson shot and killed Charlie Kirk because he disagreed with him,” McBride explained. “And he did it by firing a high-powered rifle into a group of thousands who surrounded Charlie Kirk literally on all sides. And he, like any rational person, knew that that would create a great risk of death.”

The prosecution concluded by asking the judge to bind over all of the charges.

Defense argues against bindover

Staci Visser with the defense began closing arguments by arguing against the aggravating circumstances brought against Robinson. She pointed to other cases where the circumstances actually created a great risk of death, rather than just a “possibility.”

The defense stated that “expanding the aggravator” in a case where only one person was harmed to include potential danger to a crowd went against Robinson’s constitutional rights, citing previous court hearings and cases in Utah.

Visser went on to push back on the arguments presented by the prosecution, calling much of its narrative “speculation” rather than inferences. The defense attorney poked holes in several pieces of evidence, including the screwdriver, targets found at Robinson’s home, and the additional bullets in the rifle.

She noted that when Charlie Kirk was shot, the bullet impacted the left side of his neck. Someone was standing on his right, but nobody was on his left or behind the tent, meaning there was no great risk of death.

“There’s nothing in this evidence showing a great risk that someone else was going to be killed,” Visser said.

Another member of Robinson’s defense team, Michael Burt, approached to push back on the prosecution’s arguments for bindover. He noted that they did not mention any of the three experts called by the defense, who called DNA and other evidence into question.

Burt claimed that the prosecution only put forward the reports they wanted to, rejecting questions on their reliability while claiming that Robinson’s “DNA was all over” pieces of evidence.

The defense went on to poke holes in the prosecution’s interpretation of DNA and ballistics reports based on testimony from the witnesses during the preliminary hearing.

Defense attorney Richard Novak then took over arguments, explaining that he would be arguing against bindover.

Novak looked at the obstruction of justice charge, brought against Robinson for telling Twiggs not to talk to police. The defense noted that right before he turned himself in, Robinson told his partner to ask for a lawyer and stay silent, which is a constitutional right.

At that point, Robinson had already decided to surrender and was acting out of altruism, not obstruction, Novak argued. He told Twiggs to be polite and respectful but ask for an attorney.

The defense also pushed back on the claim that Robinson had motive to kill Kirk based on political beliefs. He acknowledged Robinson’s statement, “I had enough of his hatred. Some hate can’t be negotiated out,” but said there was no evidence that Robinson’s hatred would lead him to kill.

Following Novak’s arguments, the court entered a brief afternoon recess and will return after 3:15 p.m.

Prosecution’s rebuttal

After the brief break, the prosecution took the stage for their rebuttal. Chad Grunander argued that the fact that Robinson hit his intended target did not erase the danger it posed to surrounding individuals, stating that a small degree of error could have led to the death or injury of any of the thousands of bystanders.

Grunander said that if Robinson flinched even a quarter of an inch, it would have translated to a difference of 41.8 inches between where the bullet hit and where it could have hit. He said, “This was a fluid target and that adds to the danger.”

Additionally, due to the hard materials in UVU’s courtyard, Grunander argues that the bullet could have ricocheted or caused shrapnel to hit others. This was also possible if the bullet passed through Charlie Kirk’s neck, he argued. Grunander said, “He was gambling with the lives of others…He was playing a game of ballistic roulette.”

Grunander added that Robinson came prepared with additional bullets, indicating he was prepared to have to make multiple shots and knew he may miss his first shot.

In regard to the DNA evidence, Grunander argued that DNA profiles on the rifle and its parts had DNA profiles of four individuals. He said results indicated it was one trillion times more likely to have originated from Tyler Robinson and three unknown individuals, than four unknown individuals. This creates a “reasonable inference” that Robinson’s DNA was all over the evidence.

He added that the question of this hearing is whether or not evidence supports a “reasonable belief” that Robinson created “a great risk of death” to others besides Kirk, not to prove beyond a reasonable doubt. That matter, Grunander says, is one that is reserved for trial.

He added, “The state would take this matter to trial tomorrow if it could.”

Following the prosecution’s rebuttal, the court said they would enter a recess for one hour and come back at 5 p.m. He added that the court is determined to issue a ruling today.

What happened during the initial preliminary hearing?

On day one, the court heard testimony from a UVU Police officer who was at the scene of the shooting and a Utah State Bureau of Investigation agent who gathered footage and additional evidence after the shooting.

During the second day of the hearing, State Bureau of Investigation agents testified regarding evidence; doorbell and surveillance footage were displayed; and the defense called the forensic examiner who reviewed the screwdriver and towel found at the crime scene to testify.

The third day of the hearing began with testimony from another State Bureau of Investigation agent, and the attorneys argued over the admissibility of the recorded interview with Lance Twiggs, Robinson’s former roommate and romantic partner.

Testimony of Tyler Robinson’s former romantic partner played, photos of rifle and engraved bullets shown

The victim’s representative attorney for Erika Kirk argued that the family believes the video should be played in its entirety for transparency. In response, defense attorney Richard Novak stated that the “number one concern in this proceeding is Mr. Robinson’s right to a fair trial.”

On the fourth day of the preliminary hearing, the recorded interview with Twiggs was played for the court, and messages between Robinson and Twiggs shortly after the shooting were reviewed.

Following this, the prosecution reviewed photos of the rifle, engraved bullet casings, and other evidence located during the shooting investigation.

At the end of the hearing, the parties decided that arguments before bindover would be handled in briefings. Oral arguments would be held on Sept. 1, and the judge would make his final decision on probable cause then.
