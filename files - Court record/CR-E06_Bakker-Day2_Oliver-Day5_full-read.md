### 2.2 The likelihood ratio of one

The defence put to her the STRmix run comparing **Twiggs alone** to item 7-1 (defence exhibit "Baker 38," pp. 39–44, dated 2025-09-15). Inputs as walked through: **number of contributors ASSUMED = 2**; known contributor under HP = Twiggs's known sample; **known contributors under HD = left blank.**

- Q: "what was the likelihood ratio that you calculated based on this chart?" — A: "**The likelihood ratio was one.**"
- Q: "So that doesn't rise to the level of even limited inclusion, correct?" — A: "**Correct. It would fall under an uninformative.**"

**Her explanation, in full, and it is the most important answer in her testimony:**

> "**I didn't utilize this calculation to determine that Mr. Twiggs was a possible contributor** or that I could use him as an assumed contributor. **I visually looked at the DNA sample**… We don't have a threshold. I don't have to see two, four, eight peaks to determine I think an individual is present **given that the field submitted him as an elimination**… I assumed he was a contributor. When I compared him to that data, I did see his DNA present. So when I calculated the statistic for Mr. Robinson, **I used Mr. Twiggs as an elimination.**"

⚠ **She refused the "excluded" framing, and that cuts for the State.** Asked a compound question — whether the STRmix result meant Twiggs "was not a contributor, **or** that it was not even of limited value in including him" — **she answered only the second limb.** A likelihood ratio of 1 is uninformative; **it is not evidence of absence.**

**The verbal scale (State's Exhibit 31, p. 5):** ≥ 1,000,000 = "very strong support for inclusion"; **2 is the floor for even "limited support for an inclusion"**; **1 is "uninformative."** ⚠ **As transcribed she assigned "very strong support for inclusion" to two adjacent bands** — internally inconsistent as recorded, and resolvable only against page five of the report, not against the transcript.
