# CR-E01 — Preliminary Hearing Day 4: Sworn Testimony of ATF Firearms Examiner Samantha Karner

**File ID:** CR-E01
**Scope:** the **sworn testimony of Samantha E.A. Karner**, ATF Firearm and Toolmark Examiner and author of the laboratory report of 2025-09-17, given on **Day 4** of the preliminary hearing in *State of Utah v. Tyler James Robinson*. This is the project's first file built on **Tier 1 sworn testimony**. It covers Karner's testimony from her swearing-in (06:23:51 on the transcript clock) to approximately 07:21. **It does not cover Day 4 as a whole** — see §1 for exactly what was and was not read.
**Case context:** Case No. 251403576, Fourth Judicial District Court, Provo — Hon. **Tony F. Graf, Jr.** **Tyler Robinson is presumed innocent**; as of **2026-07-27** he has **not been bound over for trial** (probable-cause ruling reserved; oral argument 2026-09-01). A preliminary hearing tests probable cause only. Judge Graf twice reminded counsel of that limit during this testimony. Nothing below is a finding of fact by any court, and nothing is adopted by this project.
**Prepared:** July 27, 2026
**Status:** v1.2 — **exhibit-numbering warning added at §3.6** on independent audit (CR-AUDIT-2026-07-28 §2.1): this file's "Exhibit 19" is the **defence's** unadmitted bullet-comparison photographs, **not** the State's Exhibit 19 (the chamber cartridge case) used in CR-E02. *(v1.1:)* **§3.6 added**, closing v1.0's largest declared gap: the State's cross-examination of Karner has now been read, and it substantially rehabilitates the methodology while producing an answer that bears directly on the defense's own bullet-fragment motion.

**Headline result:** Karner's sworn testimony **confirms the report** read at CR-ACQ §3.1 and adds four things the report alone could not give:

1. **She herself used pliers** on the bullet jacket fragment — "I cleaned the bullet jacket fragment and I also peeled the petals back… I use pliers." The manipulation the defense motion warned about had **already been performed by the ATF**, exactly as the State's notice suspected.
2. **The inconclusive result was driven by missing data, not by dissimilarity.** The fragment had "microscopic marks of value worthy of comparison," she observed **areas of agreement** with the test fires, and the obstacle was damage — "almost like missing data points." Defense counsel himself put the distinction on the record: *"Missing is not the same thing as dissimilar though, right?"*
3. **A previously unrecorded evidence-count discrepancy:** Karner was alerted that a **medical examiner's photograph appears to show seven fragments**, while the ATF received **four** lead fragments plus the jacket. **New Register D-49** (§6).
4. **The `source identification` on the cartridge case was reaffirmed under oath** — and simultaneously bounded by the DOJ's own limiting language, which the **defense** walked her through point by point.

## 2. THE WITNESS

**Samantha E.A. Karner** — Firearm and Toolmark Examiner, **ATF Forensic Science Laboratory – Washington** (Beltsville, MD); a little over **six years** at that laboratory, assigned to the Firearm and Toolmark Unit. Author of the report at CR-ACQ §3.1 (admitted as **State's Exhibit 32**). Her **47-page case file** was admitted over the State's objection as **Karner 6**; the DOJ Uniform Language policy was admitted as **Karner 11**.

**Her assigned scope, in her words:** "I received a firearm, cartridge case, three cartridges, one bullet jacket fragment, and four lead fragments. I was asked to test fire the firearm and compare the test fires to the cartridge case and the bullet jacket fragment. I also was asked to do a toolmark characterization on the cartridge case and the cartridges. And then I also ran a general rifling characteristic search on the bullet jacket fragment."

### 3.2 The pliers — the defense motion's premise, confirmed at source

> **Burt:** "…did you do certain procedures on the larger bullet fragment?"
> **Karner:** "I cleaned the bullet jacket fragment and I also peeled the petals back. If you think of a banana and peeling the skin away from the interior, it's almost like doing that… in order for me to see the land and groove impressions, which holds those individual characteristics needed for a comparison, I have to do some maneuvering of those petals."
> **Burt:** "…Do you use an instrument to peel back the portions that you want to look at?"
> **Karner:** "**I use pliers.**"

**Analyst layer.** CR-ACQ §3.2 recorded, from the State's own notice, that the ATF examiner "**may have** already folded back pieces." That hedge is now resolved: **she did, and she says so under oath** — as a documented, photographed part of the standard preparation, with before-and-after images in her case file (pp. 14–15). This **strengthens the factual premise** of the defense's motion about further FBI manipulation while saying nothing about whether that motion should have been granted. **Confidence: High.**

### 3.3 What "inconclusive" actually rested on — the most consequential exchange

Karner reaffirmed the report's conclusion verbatim, then explained its basis:

> "There was not sufficient agreement or sufficient disagreement. So yes, there was times that I saw agreement in the striations in the land and groove impressions also seeing disagreement, but there was also **a lot of lack of microscopic marks of value to see it all**. So because of this lack of quality and quantity of marks to be able to see, it never reached the threshold of sufficient agreement nor sufficient disagreement."

Walking the comparison photographs (case file pp. 37–38, test fire vs. 6A at 30× and 40×), she identified **areas of agreement** — "those two highlighted lines at the top" — and attributed the rest to damage:

> "…there's a lot of damage that is observed and almost no microscopic marks of value can be observed at all… **It's almost like missing data points.**"

Pressed repeatedly for areas of *disagreement*, she offered only "the possibility of an area of disagreement" in one image, qualified as unresolvable from that snapshot. **Defense counsel drew the distinction himself:**

> **Burt:** "Missing is not the same thing as dissimilar though, right?"
> **Karner:** "It just means there's nothing there to call agreement or disagreement."

**Analyst layer — this is the finding that most changes the picture.** The inconclusive result is a **data-sufficiency** outcome, not a **dissimilarity** outcome. On the marks that survived, the examiner reported agreement; what defeated a conclusion was damage destroying comparable surface. **That substantially weakens any reading of "inconclusive" as pointing toward a different weapon**, and it is the natural next question after CR-ACQ §3.1. **Confidence: High** as to what she testified. **Whether her interpretation is correct is an expert dispute this project does not adjudicate** — and the defense has retained its own expert (CR-ACQ §3.2).

**Recorded for the other direction, per the both-directions rule:** Karner also agreed with a definition Burt had put to her before the hearing — that an inconclusive result "indicates that the microscopic marks in question **may or may not** have originated from the same or known source." Both things are true at once, and a fair account states both.

### 3.4 The DOJ limiting language — elicited by the defense, agreed by the witness

Burt walked Karner through the DOJ *Uniform Language for Testimony and Reports* (Karner 11), and she agreed she is **not** asserting: that a source identification rests on **uniqueness**; that any conclusion is an **"individualization"**; that two toolmarks share a source **to the exclusion of all other sources**; that the discipline is **infallible**; that it has a **zero error rate**; any **statistical or numerical probability**; or **absolute/100% certainty**. She also confirmed: "I have not looked at every firearm that's been manufactured in the world."

On the cartridge case she drew the line herself:

> "My conclusion for one of the exhibits **identifies the cartridge case as being fired in the firearm, but that's all it says**."

**Analyst layer.** This does **not** retract the `source identification`; it states its epistemic status. Both readings are available on this record and both should be carried: the identification is the framework's **strongest positive conclusion**, *and* it is expressly an examiner's opinion rather than a claim of uniqueness or certainty. **Any account giving only one of those halves is incomplete** — the same error CR-ACQ v1.0 made in the other direction.

### 3.5 Points the defense scored on methodology

Recorded as **the defense's line of attack**, not as established defect:
- Karner **did not measure** the height, width, or depth of the **striae** (as distinct from the land and groove impression widths), though her subclass finding describes striae varying in exactly those dimensions.
- Her SOP contains **no specified criteria** for distinguishing subclass from individual characteristics — "No, there is not" — the judgment rests on examiner training.
- She was **unaware** the rifle was reportedly manufactured in **Germany in 1938** and had not reviewed an ATF agent's report on that issue, having agreed it "is helpful to know" a barrel's manufacturing process.

**Confidence: High** as to the exchanges; **no view taken** on whether they undermine the conclusions. Judge Graf twice redirected this line toward probable cause.

### 3.6 THE STATE'S CROSS-EXAMINATION — added v1.1, and it changes the balance

v1.0 flagged the State's examination as the largest gap in this file. It has now been read (Mr. McBride, 82 turns, from ≈07:36). **It substantially rehabilitates the methodology**, and one answer bears directly on the defense's own motion.

**The three comparison photographs — context that reverses their force.** Burt closed his direct by showing Karner three bullet comparisons (**the defence's Exhibit 19, pp. 26–28** — see the exhibit-numbering warning below), drawing agreement that individual characteristics and areas of agreement were visible, then asking: *"Would it surprise you if I told you that all three of those were fired from different weapons?"* She declined to conclude from photographs alone. On cross, McBride established what those images were: an **AFTE proficiency-test compilation, published around 2024, of photographs volunteered by examiners who had given an *incorrect* answer.** McBride then established the disanalogy:

> **McBride:** "…when you do an examination or a comparison, are you limited to one photograph?" — **Karner:** "No."
> **McBride:** "You actually have the item in front of you… you can manipulate it, you can turn it, you can look at every angle…" — **Karner:** "Yes, that's correct."
> **McBride:** "Would it be responsible for you to reach a conclusion based on a single photograph of a very limited area of two bullets?" — **Karner:** "I would not feel comfortable doing that, no."

**Analyst layer.** The defense demonstration showed that *photographs* of known-different bullets can display apparent agreement. It did **not** show that Karner's *examination* was unreliable, because her method is holistic across every land and groove impression with the item in hand. **Recorded in both directions:** the demonstration is a real illustration of examiner error existing in the discipline; it is not evidence of error here.

**Qualifications (T1, sworn).** Six and a half years at ATF FSL–Washington; previously a latent fingerprint technician recovering prints from improvised explosive devices, and a crime scene investigator in Winston-Salem, NC and Anne Arundel County, MD (~5–6 years total). **B.S. Forensic and Investigative Science, West Virginia University**; graduate of the year-long **National Firearms Examiner Academy**, where she now **teaches** (test firing; mini moot court; comparison of bullets and cartridge cases). Completed supervised casework ~2022–23. **AFTE** member; attends NIST-involved research seminars.

**Accreditation and proficiency.** The laboratory is **ANAB**-accredited — "an international standard of reporting, testimony, training and proficiency," audited every four years externally plus internal annual audits. She is **required to take proficiency tests** in every discipline within her scope.

**On PCAST.** Asked about published criticism of the field, Karner testified that AFTE "takes those recommendations very seriously. If the PCAST asked for more specific design test studies, they said, 'Yes, we will do that.'" **Recorded as her testimony, not as an assessment of whether PCAST's concerns are in fact resolved** — a contested question this project does not adjudicate. Judge Graf permitted limited recross on the point, finding the State had opened the door.

**Test-fire method.** Ammunition matched to caliber, fired into a **water recovery tank** to slow the bullet and cause minimal damage, so that comparison surfaces are preserved. The rifle is confirmed **bolt action**.

**The answer that matters most — and it cuts toward the State on the defense's own motion:**

> **McBride:** "Could further testing be done to try and see if… you come to a conclusion on this round?"
> **Karner:** "There was no further testing that I could do, no."
> **McBride:** "Is there further testing that could be done in the field?"
> **Karner:** "**There is the possibility of using a 3D system to visualize the individual characteristics on the bullet.**"

**Analyst layer — cross-file significance.** The "3D system" is the **Virtual Comparison Microscopy** the FBI proposed and the defense moved to block or condition (CR-ACQ §3.2). So the State elicited from the defense's own witness that **the contested testing is the one technique that might resolve the inconclusive result.** That reframes the motion: it is not a dispute about whether to destroy useless evidence, but about the terms on which the one potentially dispositive test proceeds. **Neither side's position is endorsed here** — the ruling remains unobtained.

## 4. THE FRAGMENT-COUNT DISCREPANCY

> **Burt:** "Were you informed at some point that there was a discrepancy between what you were examining and what had been documented by the medical examiner, in terms of the number of bullet fragments?"
> **Karner:** "I was, yes."
> **Burt:** "…was it your understanding that there were originally seven bullet fragments, but you ended up with four?"
> **Karner:** "**I was alerted that there was potentially seven fragments from the medical examiner in a photo just a few months ago.**"

She also testified the evidence arrived **sealed**, in "a small individual manila envelope," and that she photographed the contents as received (case file p. 14, showing 6A through 6E). Asked whether she had been asked to check if the ATF lost any of it: "**No, I was not.**"

**Analyst layer.** Two counts are in tension: a medical examiner's photograph said to show **potentially seven** fragments, and **five** items received at ATF (jacket 6A + four lead 6B–6E). **What this establishes:** that Karner was told of the discrepancy and that it remains unexplained on the portion of the record read. **What it does not establish:** that anything was lost, mishandled, or concealed — the seal was intact at her end, "potentially seven" is a hedge about a photograph, and the counting conventions behind the two figures may simply differ. **This is a genuine open question, not an allegation.** → **D-49**.
