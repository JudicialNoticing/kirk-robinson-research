## §3 · RECORDING LAYER — what each side asserted, attributed and not adopted

### 3.1 The defence's case (Dkt. 250, 293, 348)

- **R-1.** A deputy prosecutor on the trial team — **`[Dpty Atty]`, whose name the State redacted and which this library declines to reconstruct (§7.1)** — has a concurrent conflict under **URPC 1.7(a)(2)** arising from a personal connection to a person present at the 2025-09-10 event; the UCAO employed **no screening protocol**; the information was shared within the prosecution team including the County Attorney; therefore the **entire office** should be disqualified. *(Dkt. 250 p.2.)*
- **R-2.** Disclosure chronology asserted: **2025-09-29** the prosecutor asks a UCAO investigator for help; **2025-09-30** they measure at UVU; **2025-10-01** he discloses by email to defence counsel; **2025-10-09** a call with defence. ⚠️ **Note the order the defence draws attention to — the measurement preceded the disclosure by one day.**
- **R-3.** The death-penalty notice was filed **2025-09-16**, the same day as the Information and *"[l]ess than one week after"* the messages reached the County Attorney, though not due until 60 days after arraignment; the defence calls this *"[t]he rush to seek death"* evidencing *"strong emotional reactions."* **Attributed argument.**
- **R-4.** Legal theory: imputation to the whole office under ***State v. McClellan***, 2009 UT 50 — which the defence says disqualified **this same office** where there was no attempt to screen — plus *Balfour*, *Connor*, *Tate*; due process under *Marshall v. Jerrico* and *Ganger v. Peyton*; Eighth Amendment heightened-reliability framing throughout.
- **R-5 (Dkt. 348).** A **second, independent** conflict is asserted against County Attorney **Jeffrey Gray personally**: that in sworn testimony on 2026-01-16 he revealed that children of two members of his congregation were also present at the event, one *"shook up"*; that he learned of it only after the Information was filed; that he *"kept it secret until he was asked about it while testifying under oath"*; and that he acknowledged not reporting the scenario to the AG's office. ⚠️ **All of this is the defence's characterisation of a transcript this library does not hold** (§9 A-1).
- **R-6 (Dkt. 348).** Proposed standard for triggering § 17-68-304 in a capital case: *"no more than a **non-frivolous factual predicate**"*, citing *Ford v. Wainwright*. The filing records that **the court itself asked the parties what standard applies** and that the Utah Code does not answer it.
- **R-7 (Dkt. 348).** Defence concedes the court *"expressed concern"* that its oral referral request took the court and opposing counsel by surprise and **takes full responsibility**; characterises the State's "stalling tactic" response as *"disingenuous"*; and complains of *"increasingly prejudicial media coverage."*

### 3.2 The State's case (Dkt. 289, 366)

- **R-8.** The parties agree on the law; the dispute is **application**. The test is URPC 1.7(a)(2)'s *"significant risk"* that representation *"will be materially limited … by a personal interest of the lawyer,"* and **the burden is the defendant's**.
- **R-9.** **`[AC]`**, an 18-year-old student, stood at the far-right upper edge of the amphitheatre, **~85 feet** from Kirk; **there was no line of sight** between that position and the shooter's position on the Losee Center roof, because the Sorensen Center intervened; `[AC]` would have had to move **75 feet southeast** to establish one.
- **R-10.** `[AC]` did not see the shooting, the wound, or anyone with a gun; nearly all of `[AC]`'s knowledge is hearsay; the testimony would be *"duplicative"* and *"obtainable from literally thousands."* Under URPC 3.7's logic, disqualification is not required where potential testimony is unnecessary or relates to an uncontested issue.
- **R-11.** At the moment of the shooting `[Dpty Atty]` was with County Attorney Gray **at a conference in Ogden**; he learned of it from `[AC]`'s message and shared it with Gray; he left 10–15 minutes later for the UVU command post, Gray following ~20 minutes later.
- **R-12.** Four institutional reasons are given for the same-day death notice — the strength of the aggravated-murder evidence; a campaign commitment made ~3 years earlier; a lesson from the pending *State v. Jayne* matter that delay *"only fuels speculation and misinformation"*; and a wish to minimise speculation here. Three same-day comparators are offered (*Jayne*, *Bate*, *Roman*).
- **R-13.** On imputation: *"it is usually sufficient to disqualify the particular attorney … rather than the entire office"* (*Balfour* ¶34, resting on *Bolden*), and disqualifying government attorneys *"implicates separation of powers issues."* Every defence authority is distinguished on its facts.
- **R-14.** A prosecutor may properly consider *"the potential collateral impact on third parties, including witnesses or victims"* (ABA Standards § 3-4.4(x)) — an affirmative argument that the interest is **public, not personal**.
- **R-15 (Dkt. 366).** § 17-68-304 lets an *overwhelmed* county attorney enlist the AG; a court may only *recommend* that he seek assistance, and the AG may only *assist*, never replace. Separation of powers (Utah Const. art. V § 1) forbids a court dictating which elected prosecutor prosecutes. ⚠️ **The State also states a scoped null of its own:** apart from *Jiminez*, it *"could not find any case where section 17-68-304, or its predecessors, was invoked in any situation."*
- **R-16 (Dkt. 366).** *"The undisputed facts of this public assassination dictated the charging decisions here, not tangential personal relationships."* And a **direct factual denial**: as to the assertion that Gray made *"similar public pronouncements,"* — *"The County Attorney made no such 'similar public pronouncements.'"*

### 6.3 A contemporaneous private message record of the first 50 minutes
