### 2.1 The Motion to Preserve Evidence chain, in sequence

| Date | Filing | Substance |
|---|---|---|
| **2025-10-08** | **Defendant's Motion to Preserve Evidence** (Dkt. #75, 3 pp., `/s/` Nester) | Asks the court to order the Utah County Attorney's Office **"to preserve all evidence in its custody or under its control,"** including that of any law-enforcement agency involved, **and to notify defence counsel prior to any potentially destructive testing and/or examination**, citing U.C.A. §§ 77-11c-303(3), 77-11c-401(5)(a), 77-11c-301, 77-11c-302, U.R.Cr.P. 16, the Fifth, Sixth, Eighth and Fourteenth Amendments and Utah Const. art. I §§ 1, 7, 12 |
| **2025-10-20** | *(Order granting the motion — Dkt. #107)* | **Not held.** Its existence and premature entry are established by the two filings below |
| **2025-10-22** | **State's Motion to Set Aside** (3 pp., `/s/` Ballard) | The 10-20 order was entered **before the State's response deadline had run** (Utah R. Civ. P. 7(d)(1)) and **without any Request to Submit** (Utah R. Crim. P. 12(b)). The State argues the court *"committed a legal error"* and *"abused its discretion,"* citing *State v. Torres-Orellana*, 2024 UT 46 |
| **2025-10-22** | **State's Response** (Dkt. #117, 7 pp.) | Agrees a general duty to preserve **material** evidence exists (*California v. Trombetta*; U.R.Cr.P. 16(a)(1)(F)) but **opposes "preserve all evidence" regardless of materiality**, proposing a release mechanism; and **opposes any requirement to notify the defence before potentially destructive testing**, citing *State v. Bakalov*, 1999 UT 45, and Utah Code § 77-11c-401(3)(a) for the proposition that *"due process is not implicated merely because forensic testing will consume a piece of evidence"* |
| **2025-11-04** | ⚠️ **STIPULATED ORDER** (4 pp., signed Graf, court seal noted) | **(1)** Sets aside the 10-20 order. **(2)** Grants the motion **in part**, ordering preservation of **"all evidence"** in the custody or control of the County Attorney, its agents and any involved law-enforcement agency. **(3)** Permits release or disposal only on notice to defence counsel and **no objection within 14 days**, or agreement. **(4)** **ORDERS notice to defence counsel PRIOR TO any potentially destructive testing and/or examination**, with the same 14-day objection window — and **defines the term** |

⚠️ **THE DEFINITION, VERBATIM, because the library will need it repeatedly:**
> *"Testing and/or examination is potentially destructive only when it is likely to entirely consume a piece of evidence, or an extract from that piece of evidence, or is likely to change the character of a piece of evidence."*

### 2.3 The Pretrial Protective Order (Dkt. #14, 2025-09-16, 6 pp.)

A **standard-form pretrial protective order under Utah Code § 78B-7-803**, signed by Judge Graf on 2025-09-16. It names **Erika Kirk** as the protected person, categorised on the form as *"not an intimate partner or cohabitant, but is an alleged victim."* The form records the finding that *"The defendant represents a credible threat to the physical safety of the protected person,"* and imposes standard no-abuse, no-contact, residence-exclusion and stay-away provisions.

## §4 · Findings

**F-27 — THE GOVERNING PRESERVATION AND NOTICE ORDER POSTDATES THE CONSUMPTION THIS LIBRARY HAS SPENT MOST OF ITS DNA WORK ON.**
Grade: **High.** The order requiring notice before potentially destructive testing was entered **2025-11-04**. The FBI's testing ran from **2025-09-11** at "Priority 1 … 24 hours a day," and ATF was assigned **2025-09-12**; the consumption recorded at CR-E06 and DNA-E01 — six FBI items entirely consumed with no DNA obtained, nine ATF items entirely consumed — **occurred weeks before any such order existed.**
**Why it matters:** DNA-E01 §6.2 records, and expressly leaves open, whether an attorney was assigned to Mr. Robinson as at 2025-09-12 and whether any was contacted before consumption. **This finding does not answer that question — it reframes it.** The ATF analyst's testimony that her laboratory requests that agents *"reach out to an attorney if one is assigned"* describes a **laboratory practice**, not compliance with a court order, because no court order then required it.
⚠️ **Guard, running toward the State and stated as a finding rather than a footnote: nothing here establishes any impropriety.** Consuming evidence before a preservation order exists is not a breach of that order, and the State's litigation position was that **due process does not require pre-testing notice at all** (*Bakalov*). **The chronology is recorded; no inference of fault is drawn, and none may be drawn from here.**

**F-28 — The State argued that pre-testing notice was not required, and then stipulated to it.**
Grade: **High.** Its 2025-10-22 response says the State *"should not be required to notify Defendant of any potentially destructive forensic testing"* and cites *Bakalov* for the proposition that consumption alone does not implicate due process. Thirteen days later the State **stipulated** to an order that requires exactly that notice, with a 14-day objection window.
⚠️ **This is recorded as a litigation fact, not as a criticism.** Stipulating to a term one argued against is ordinary practice and may reflect settlement of a low-stakes dispute. **What it establishes for this library is narrower and useful: the notice requirement is a NEGOTIATED term of a stipulated order, not a judicial holding on what due process requires** — so no file may cite it as the court's ruling on that question.

**F-29 — The "Motion to Preserve Evidence" is NOT the bullet-jacket-fragment motion, and Q-35 is not closed by these documents.**
Grade: **High.** The banked ballistics filing is *"DEFENDANT'S MOTION FOR AN ORDER PROHIBITING ANY FURTHER ALTERATION OR TESTING OF A BULLET JACKET FRAGMENT…"* — 19 pages. The preservation motion is **3 pages** and general in scope. They are different filings addressing different problems, and the preservation chain does not mention the fragment.
⚠️ **Recorded expressly because the titles invite the conflation, and this file nearly made it.** **Q-35 — the ruling on the bullet-fragment motion and the State's response to it — remains open**, and neither document appears in the portion of project 224251 read so far.

**F-30 — The "Pretrial Protective Order" is a victim no-contact order, NOT the pretrial publicity order the 2026-06-26 contempt ruling enforced.**
Grade: **High.** Docket #14 is a form order under **Utah Code § 78B-7-803**, a domestic-violence-and-victim protection provision, naming a protected person and imposing no-contact and stay-away terms **on the defendant**. The contempt matter concerned a **pretrial publicity order** constraining **counsel's** public statements (CR-ACQ-2026-08-14, Q-65).
⚠️ **Two instruments, similar names, opposite subjects — and this file assumed they were the same before reading.** **No file may cite docket #14 for anything to do with the contempt matter.** The publicity order itself **remains unheld** and is a live acquisition target.
