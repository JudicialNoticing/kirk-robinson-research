### 2.0 Introduction (pp. 1–3)

The State opens: *"Defendant does not challenge the substance of the evidence demonstrating that he was the person who crawled to the sniper's perch on the Losee Building rooftop, fired the fatal shot that killed Mr. Charlie Kirk, hid the rifle in a wooded area, got rid of some of the clothing he was wearing, and told his roommate to delete their texts about the shooting"* (p. 1). It asserts the defence *"merely asserts the repeatedly rejected claim"* that this evidence *"could not be introduced as reliable hearsay,"* and therefore *"raises no valid challenge"* to **Count 1 "as at least murder," Count 2 (felony discharge of a firearm causing serious bodily injury), Counts 3 and 4 (obstruction of justice), or Count 5 (tampering with a witness)** (pp. 1–2).

🚨 **The defence's threshold Rule 1102 hearsay argument — the lead argument of the brief being replied to (CR-E07 §2.0) — receives NO numbered argument section in the reply.** Measured with capable patterns, both readings: `1102` = **0**; `hearsay` = **1** (the Introduction sentence above). The reply's entire treatment of the threshold challenge is that one clause.

The Introduction also previews: the aggravator challenge rests on *"misrepresentation of both the evidentiary standard … and the test"*; **an accusation that defence counsel *"have therefore violated their ethical duty of candor to the Court"*** (p. 2 — §2.1 below); challenges to Counts 6 and 7 *"improperly draw inferences in Defendant's favor"*; and the defence *"misrepresents the holding in* Apprendi *v. New Jersey"* (p. 3).

⚠️ **Count-numbering observation, recorded for verification against the held Information (CR-E08):** the Introduction names **Count 5 "tampering with a witness"** and separately **Count 6 "witness tampering for directing Mr. Twiggs not to talk to police"** — two tampering-labelled counts. ### 2.1 Argument I — the evidentiary standard, and the candor accusation (pp. 3–6)

The State argues the defence *"relies on an erroneous distinction between inferences and speculation"* from ***State v. Cristobal*, 2010 UT App 228, ¶ 16, 238 P.3d 1096** that the Utah Supreme Court *"soundly rejected"* in ***State v. Ashcraft*, 2015 UT 5, ¶¶ 21-24, 349 P.3d 664**; that the Court of Appeals has *"expressly and repeatedly recognized"* the repudiation (***State v. Wall*, 2020 UT App 36, ¶ 54** — *"we no longer view Cristobal's statement regarding equally plausible inferences as valid"* per ***State v. Law*, 2020 UT App 74, ¶ 22**; ***State v. Oseguera-Lopez*, 2020 UT App 115, ¶ 32**); and that at a preliminary hearing the magistrate *"must 'view all evidence in the light most favorable to the prosecution' and 'draw all reasonable inferences in favor of the prosecution'"* (***State v. Schmidt*, 2015 UT 65, ¶ 4**, quoting ***State v. Maughan*, 2013 UT 37, ¶ 14**; also ***State v. Ramirez*, 2012 UT 59, ¶ 9***). On the inference/speculation line it quotes ***State v. Tran*, 2024 UT 7, ¶ 35** (quoting *Heslop v. Bear River Mut. Ins. Co.*, 2017 UT 5, ¶ 22; *Salt Lake City v. Carrera*, 2015 UT 73, ¶ 12).

🚨 **The candor accusation, verbatim in its operative sentence (p. 5):** *"This blatant omission violates counsel's duty of candor to the court. See Utah R. Prof'l Conduct § 3.3(a)(2)."* The predicate is the assertion that the defence quoted and cited *Cristobal* (Def.'s Mem. at 11, 20-21) while *"complet[ing] ignor[ing]"* *Ashcraft* and the decisions recognising its repudiation of *Cristobal*. **This is the State's allegation about opposing counsel's professional conduct. It is recorded as such and adopted nowhere** (→ F-126; §3 A-5).

### 2.2 Argument II — the great-risk-of-death (KGRD) aggravator (pp. 6–14)

**II.A (test):** the State argues ***State v. Sosa-Hurtado*, 2019 UT 65, ¶ 39 n.9, 455 P.3d 63** *"expressly stated that it was 'not establishing a multifactor balancing test'"* and that *"[t]he ultimate test is the statutory test"*; that the legislature *"has already narrowed the aggravator"*; that *"high probability of death"* language *"is just another way to say a 'great risk' of death"*; and that fact-pattern comparisons to other cases are beside the point. It distinguishes *Sosa-Hurtado*'s own facts (three shots, two victims) as raising a scope question this case does not (*"this case involves only the murderous act itself,"* p. 9, citing ***State v. Pierre*, 572 P.2d 1338, 1355 (Utah 1977)** via *Sosa-Hurtado*).
