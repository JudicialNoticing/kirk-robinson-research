> ### ⚠ CROSS-REFERENCE ADDED v1.2 — 12.1's ADMISSION ON DAY 2 WAS THE STATE'S **SECOND** ATTEMPT
>
> **Nothing in this section is retracted; a reader simply cannot tell from it why State's Exhibit 12.4 exists.** It exists **because 12.1 failed the day before.** On **Day 1** 12.1 was offered and the defence's authenticity objection was **SUSTAINED — it was NOT admitted** — because although the Rule 1102 statements covered the *original* recordings, **nobody accounted for who made the zooms, circles and face-blurring**. The State undertook on the record to build an unaltered version overnight, and did: **that version is 12.4.** See **CR-E05 §5** for the Day 1 exclusion and the resolution of **D-52**.
>
> **Consequences for this section, all of them additive:**
> - **12.4 is not a companion exhibit. It is the cure** — or rather, the thing that made the defect immaterial. The court itself had asked on Day 1, "do you have a version of this exhibit that does not have the zooming in or the circling?"
> - **The 12.3 row above is accurate and was NEVER amended.** ⚠ **The same statement, containing that same sentence, was already in evidence on Day 1** — the State quoted the "only changes are some zooms and red highlights" language in argument *before* the Day 1 ruling, and it did not save the exhibit. **The court held that statement, standing alone, insufficient to authenticate the altered exhibit**: 12.3 *describes* the alterations but does not come from whoever *made* them.
> - ⚠ **The Day 1 defect was never literally cured.** No one from the Utah County Attorney's Office testified and no Rule 1102 statement from the alterer was ever offered. **The court stated no basis for admitting 12.1 on Day 2** — the ruling addresses publication only, and the renewed authenticity objection is not addressed anywhere on the record.
> - **The Rule 403 non-publication ruling recorded here was NOT the end of it.** On **Day 4** counsel for the victim's family moved for reconsideration; on **Day 5** the court ordered **12.1 played in full to everyone physically present in the courtroom, with the livestream cut to audio only and still photography of the monitors barred.** **Any statement about 12.1's publication must date-stamp which ruling it means.**
>
> **This does not touch any substantive finding of this file.** The timestamps, the movement sequence, the absence of casings and the "There is not, Judge" answer are all unaffected by how the exhibit was admitted.

**The court's rulings.** **12.4 was admitted and published**, in the courtroom and to the media camera, over defense objection. **12.1 was admitted but NOT published** — Judge Graf reviewed it on his own monitor, reasoning that as it is "the exact same video… simply zooming in," publication was unnecessary, and expressly invoking Utah R. Evid. 403's "needlessly presenting cumulative evidence." Defense (Burt) argued a "realistic likelihood of prejudice" under *Kearns v. Tribune Corp.*, 685 P.2d 515, and *Allgier*, 258 P.3d 589, citing a **public-opinion survey** submitted to show the jury pool was already affected. **David Ryman** appeared for the news media.

**On the record, from the State:** asked directly by the court whether any footage captures the shooting — **"There is not, Judge."** Sturgill volunteered the clarification that the video shows the person going to the roof, to the edge, low-crawling, and leaving "after what we assume is the shot being fired. That's as close as it gets."

## 5. FINDINGS THAT CUT THE OTHER WAY

Recorded as prominently as §4, per the both-directions rule.

### 5.1 No casings or bullets at the Losee building — now direct testimony

> **Nester:** "…did they ever find any bullets or casings anywhere on the Losee roof?"
> **Hull:** "Not to my knowledge, no."
> **Nester:** "Did they ever find any bullets or casings anywhere around the Losee building?" … "Immediately around the Losee building?" — "Not to my knowledge, no."

**Analyst layer.** CR-E02 §4 **inferred** from a keyword-sampled read that no cartridge case was recovered at the scene, and CR-AUDIT §2.1 leaned on that inference to narrow D-48. **It is now direct sworn testimony from the scene commander** — subject to his "not to my knowledge" hedge and his March 2026 departure. This **raises the inference to a finding at Medium-high** and correspondingly **strengthens** the point that the ATF's `source identification` linked a case found *inside the rifle*, not one recovered at the shooting scene.

### 5.2 The .223 round on the Computer Science roof — accounted for

CR-E03 §4.1 recorded Davis's testimony about a **live .223 round on the Computer Science building roof** with no line of sight to the tent, and deliberately drew no inference. Hull supplies the explanation:

> "My recollection is that was accounted for as an **ejected cartridge from an officer who had cycled his rifle**… When a weapon is cleared, an officer would make his rifle ready, which would chamber a round."

He did **not** recall which building, the calibre, or where the item is now, and did not treat it as a scene worth preserving. **Assessment:** the two accounts are **consistent** — an unfired round ejected while an officer readied a rifle is exactly a live round on a rooftop — and the innocuous explanation is the one the record offers. **Confidence: Medium** (Hull's express non-recollection of the specifics; Davis's account came from Agent Snyder's report, unread). **Recorded so this item does not circulate as an unexplained anomaly.**

### 5.3 The rooftop footage could not identify anyone

- The roof video **as a standalone** was **not** distinguishable — no facial features, no clothing detail.
- Identification rested on **other cameras**; the images **released to the press** were from the **stairwell/parking-garage** sequence.
- There was internal discussion of "**a pattern on the front of the shirt**" seen as the individual jumped.
- **Two young men at the event recorded video of a person on the roof roughly ten seconds before the shooting** — looking north, dark clothing, at the corner of the roof. Hull's understanding was that **they believed it was "overwatch or some kind of a police overwatch for the event."** He had **not** reviewed their interview.
- On the object carried: "carrying an object in his hand that's **concealed, or in a bag**, and it is long and has the appearance, based on my training and experience, **it could be a firearm**." Asked whether an actual gun is visible in the video — **"No."**
- **No motorist reported** seeing a man with a gun crossing Campus Drive, though two cars passed directly in front of him.

**Analyst layer.** The witnesses' "police overwatch" impression is recorded because it is Tier 1 sworn testimony about a contemporaneous civilian perception, and because it is precisely the sort of detail that acquires independent life in retellings. **It establishes what two bystanders thought they were seeing; it establishes nothing about who was on the roof.** Hull had not read their interview, so this is second-hand within his own testimony. **Their video and interview are unobtained (§7).**

### 5.4 The early custody, other firearms, and the holster

- Someone was **taken into custody early** and word came down that "the individual was **not considered to be a suspect** in the shooting." Hull never interacted with that person. *(This library maintains `files - George Zinn`; **whether this refers to that matter is NOT established here** and must not be assumed.)*
- **Several people were detained for questioning** during the investigation and **all were ultimately cleared.**
- **A handgun was found in a backpack** on scene that day and accounted for.
- **Trooper Noah Gonzalez**, an off-duty trooper present that day, was involved in recovering **a holster**; shown Davis's report, Hull confirmed **"Trooper Gonzalez included in his report that there was a gun, yes."** Hull had no independent recollection.

**Recorded without inference.** Firearms at a public event in Utah are unremarkable and none of this implicates anyone. It is recorded because the library's existing files touch adjacent questions and because an incomplete account of what else was on scene is how alternative narratives get their raw material.

### 5.5 The investigative posture at the moment of surrender

- Hull's own report is dated **2025-09-14**. **Sergeant Bricker** separately prepared a timeline; Hull built his own "over the course of the investigation," not before the surrender.
- Robinson **turned himself in on 2025-09-11** (Hull's belief).
- **Nester:** "as of the moment that Tyler Robinson voluntarily surrendered… you all did not know who the shooter was. Is that right?"
  **Hull:** "**We had identified a person of interest, but we did not specifically know who the shooter was at that time.**"
- Word of the surrender reached investigators from the **Washington County Sheriff**, via the university control-centre administration.
