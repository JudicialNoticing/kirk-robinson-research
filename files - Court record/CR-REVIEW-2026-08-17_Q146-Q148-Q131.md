## §2 · Q-146 — the Count 4 offence-date divergence: **CONFIRMED as a divergence, ASSESSED as not register-worthy**

### 2.1 Both sides read directly

**The Information** (`2025-09-16_information-charging-document_dc27880532.txt`), read count by count, and **both XChange exports** (`2026-03-15_xchange-case-history-full_dc27880531.txt` and `..._short-case-history_dc27880533.txt`), which are two independent exports and **agree with each other on all seven rows**:

| | Information — the pleading's own words | XChange — the `Offense Date` field |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *"in that **on September 10, 2025**"* — flat | September 10, 2025 |
| 2 | *"in that **on September 10, 2025**"* — flat | September 10, 2025 |
| 3 | *"in that **on September 10, 2025**"* — flat | September 10, 2025 |
| **4** | *"in that **on or about September 11, 2025**"* | **September 10, 2025** ← **the one divergence** |
| 5 | *"in that **on or about September 10, 2025**"* | September 10, 2025 |
| 6 | *"in that **on or about September 11, 2025**"* | **September 11, 2025** ✅ agrees |
| 7 | *"in that **on or about September 10, 2025**"* | September 10, 2025 |

✅ **The queue's observation is correct: exactly one row diverges, and it is Count/Charge 4.** Confirmed at Tier 1 on both sides. **Confidence: High.**

### 2.2 🚨 The decisive test — and it is a "verify in both directions" act, not a confirmation

**The obvious innocent explanation is that the docket simply stamps the incident date on every charge.** That explanation is **REFUTED by the docket itself: Charge 6 carries September 11, 2025.** The field is populated per charge, it demonstrably *can* hold the later date, and one row nevertheless differs.

⚠️ **This was worth running before writing anything.** Had Charge 6 also read September 10, the entire item would have collapsed into a formatting artifact and no finding would exist.

### 2.3 ⚠️ But the steelman substantially weakens it, and it must be recorded before the item resolves

**Two facts cut against treating this as a conflict between claims:**

1. 🚨 **Count 4 is pleaded *"on or about."*** The Information distinguishes: Counts **1–3 are pleaded flat** (*"in that on September 10, 2025"*), Counts **4–7 are pleaded *"on or about."*** *"On or about"* is a deliberately approximate pleading formula; **a one-day variance is immaterial to it as a matter of pleading.** So the divergence is between a form of words chosen *because it does not fix a date* and a **typed administrative field that can only hold one value.**
2. **The two artifacts are not making the same kind of statement.** The pleading asserts what the State charges. The `Offense Date` field is a court-administration datum entered by a clerk. **This is the `metadata is not content` hazard in its ordinary form** — and the queue row said so itself.

### 2.4 Resolution — narrow, and against opening a register entry

> ✅ **Q-146 RESOLVES. The divergence is real, measured and Tier 1 on both sides. It does NOT get a D-number.**

**Why not.** A D-number records a discrepancy between sources of comparable weight *about the same proposition*. Here **one side is not asserting the proposition at all**: an *"on or about"* pleading declines to fix the date, and the docket field is not a statement by any party. Nothing in this library rests on the docket's Count-4 value. Assigning a register entry would put an administrative typo — or, just as likely, a correct clerical reading of *"on or about"* as "the incident date" — beside findings drawn from sworn testimony.

**What it gets instead:**

> **F-111 — AN OFFENCE-DATE COLUMN IS A TYPED FIELD, NOT THE PLEADING; AND WHERE THE PLEADING SAYS *"ON OR ABOUT,"* THE COLUMN CANNOT REPRESENT IT AT ALL.** The XChange export records **Charge 4 offence date September 10, 2025**; the Information pleads **Count 4 *"on or about September 11, 2025."*** Both read directly, T1. ✅ **The docket is capable of the later date — Charge 6 carries September 11** — so this is a genuine one-row divergence, not a formatting default. ⚠️ **But four of the seven counts are pleaded *"on or about,"* a formula that by design does not fix a date, so a single-valued column cannot faithfully carry them.** 🚨 **Operational rule: cite the pleading for any offence date, never the column — and where the pleading says *"on or about,"* say so, because dropping those three words converts an approximation into an assertion.** **Confidence: High.** *(Q-146.)*

**What would change this:** an amended Information, a docket correction, or any filing by either party that treats the Count-4 date as fixed. **The held docket export stops at 2026-03-14/15**, so a later correction would not be visible here.

### 2.5 ✅ One incidental confirmation, logged because confirmations must be

**The Information pleads seven counts, and the docket carries seven charges, statute for statute.** Count 4 and Charge 4 are the same charge (§ 76-8-306(2), obstruction, second degree, the clothing). **Q-140's C4 "charge count = 7" holds on a re-read.**

## §4 · Q-131 — the validator was blind, and the blindness is now measured

**`queue-check.sh` built its Q-ID list from `^\| Q-[0-9]+ \|`, which matches only UNADORNED rows.** Almost every row is now written `| **Q-NNN** |` or `| ~~Q-NN~~ |`.

| | Q-IDs seen |
|---|---|
| Old pattern | **38** |
| Bold- and strikethrough-tolerant pattern | **149** |

> 🚨 **The check was operating on 25% of the queue — it saw 38 of 149 IDs.** The queue row called it *"nearly empty"* and *"nearly blind"*; **the true figure is that three of every four items were invisible to both the duplicate check and the blocked-by existence check.** It passed only because genuine `Q-NNN (` references are rare.

✅ **Fixed as the queue row specified: the ID pattern is now `^\| *~{0,2}\*{0,2}Q-[0-9]+`, and the REFERENCE pattern is deliberately left noisy.** The comment in the script says why, so the next reader does not "improve" it: **a smarter matcher risks a false negative, which would let an unverified finding escape, and a noisy validator is the safe failure direction.**

✅ **Re-run after the fix: `Q-IDs unique (149 distinct)` · no orphaned blocked-by references · RESULT clean.** **No duplicate surfaced** — so the queue was in fact clean; it simply had not been checked.

⚠️ **Residual:** the count check itself is now a self-claim, and standing hazard 15 says self-counts in this library run one low. **149 was produced by parsing, not by reading, and has not been reconciled against a hand count.**
