## §3 · What the State argued — RECORDING LAYER, every proposition the State's claim

**The framing.** The State opens by calling this *"likely as straight-forward a bindover decision as this Court will ever see"* and closes Argument I with *"[t]his isn't a close case."* Its theory: admissions to friends establish most elements; DNA, surveillance video and eyewitness testimony supply the rest.

**The standard (Argument I).** The State argues probable cause is a *"low bar"* (*Lopez*, 2020 UT 61 ¶ 48), lower even than a preponderance (*Clark*, 2001 UT 9); that the magistrate must view all evidence and draw all reasonable inferences **in the light most favourable to the prosecution**; that the State need not *"eliminate alternative inferences … in favor of the defense"* (*Lopez* ¶ 46); and that bindover may be declined only where the facts give *"no more than a basis for speculation"* (*Lopez* ¶ 48). It cites *Anderson*, 612 P.2d 778, for the hearing's purpose as ferreting out groundless prosecutions, and *Virgin*, 2006 UT 29, for the magistrate's limited credibility role.

**Count 1, aggravated murder.** Elements pleaded as identity · intentionally or knowingly · caused the death · knowingly created a great risk of death to another (§ 76-5-202(2)(a)(iii)). Death and identity are argued from admissions, DNA on the screwdriver, towel, rifle parts and fired cartridge, the vehicle's presence, and Mr. Twiggs's confirmation that surveillance stills *"definitely"* looked like the defendant. Mental state is argued from the admissions, the engraved cartridges, and pre-shooting target practice. **The aggravator occupies the argument's largest block (pp. 11–17)** and is argued two ways: everyone in the **line of fire** (the questioner standing feet in front of Mr. Kirk, and those behind him), and everyone **near** Mr. Kirk though not in the line of fire — the latter on *Sosa-Hurtado*, 2019 UT 65, plus Pennsylvania, Florida and Arizona authority on stray, ricocheting and pass-through bullets.
> 🔍 **Two inferences the State draws expressly, because they are the argument's load-bearing joints and are inferences, not observations:** (i) that loading **four** cartridges rather than one supports an inference the shooter *anticipated missing*; and (ii) that a rifle disassembled for transport and reassembled on the roof with a screwdriver *"would not be as accurate as it previously was when it was sighted-in while in one piece."* Both are the State's reasoning. **Neither is a forensic finding, and neither is adopted here.**

**Count 2, felony discharge of a firearm causing serious bodily injury** (§ 76-11-210(2)(a)): argued as following from the Count 1 evidence, with death itself satisfying the serious-bodily-injury element.

**Counts 3 and 4, obstruction of justice:** the rifle left in a bush, the clothing changed and *"discarded"* — the latter on an admission the State attributes to **Michael Mitchell** (State's Ex. 14 at 3) — **the Mike Mitchell this library already holds as the family friend who assisted the surrender** (`CR-E22 F-6`). Intent is argued from the defendant's own messages about keeping the matter secret and leaving no evidence. *(⚠ statute-number defect — `CR-E22 F-3`.)*

**Counts 5 and 6, witness tampering** (§ 76-8-508): directing Mr. Twiggs to delete the exchange and not to speak to police.

**Count 7, violent offence in the presence of a child** (§§ 76-3-203.10; 76-3-203.14(1)(k), (2)(a)), enhanced to a class A misdemeanour by victim targeting. Child presence is argued from two videos. **The victim-targeting element is where the brief does its most contested work:** it argues expressly *against* a defence position that Mr. Kirk *"was only a religious evangelist"*, characterises TPUSA and Mr. Kirk's stated positions from State's Ex. 5.1, and sets them against the defendant's relationship with Mr. Twiggs and Mr. Twiggs's reported exploration of transition, plus the engraved-cartridge inscriptions. **This passage is the State's characterisation of a homicide victim's politics and of two living people's private lives, offered to prove a targeting element.** It is recorded here as the State's argument and is assessed nowhere in this file.

**Argument III, the sentencing enhancements on Counts 1 and 2:** the State argues the Court **need not bind over** on enhancements that do not raise the offence level, because they are not elements (*Clark*; *Ramirez*; *Schreuder*; *Angus*; *Martinez*) — and that it made the showing anyway.
