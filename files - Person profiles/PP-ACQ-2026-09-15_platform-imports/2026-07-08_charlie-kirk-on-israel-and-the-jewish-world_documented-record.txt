# Charlie Kirk on Israel and the Jewish World

A documented chronology of positions, evolution, primary evidence, and the posthumous dispute — not a eulogy and not a prosecution.

 Prepared July 8, 2026
 Positions through assassination on September 10, 2025
 Hearing record through July 2026

## Executive Summary

Charlie Kirk (1993–2025), co-founder of Turning Point USA, was assassinated on September 10, 2025, at Utah Valley University. Since his death, one of the most contested questions on the American right has been what he believed about Israel, Jewish political influence, and the pro-Israel donor networks that helped build his organization — and whether those beliefs were changing at the end of his life.

This report assembles the documented record. Its central finding is that the popular framings on both sides are incomplete. Kirk did not convert from Zionist to anti-Zionist, and he was not the unchanged, uncomplicated ally that some eulogists describe. The verified record shows directional movement across roughly a decade.

 2012–2022. Unambiguous public support for Israel — first through the donor and activist network that built TPUSA, later through evangelical faith. In this period, Israel support was the primary line of attack used against him by the antisemitic far right (the Groyper Wars).

 Late 2023. First verified cracks. Weeks after October 7, Kirk publicly blamed “Jewish donors” and secular Jewish philanthropy for funding left-wing “cultural Marxism,” while insisting the critique was pro-Israel tough love. He also floated a “stand-down order” question about October 7.

 2024–2025. Shift from advocate to coalition manager as his Gen Z audience turned against Israel. He platformed prominent critics, treated the Epstein–Mossad theory as a live hypothesis, questioned aid and AIPAC in private settings, and demanded the right to criticize Israel without being labeled antisemitic — while condemning antisemitism on the right and professing theological devotion to Israel.

 Final days. A verified public/private split. Publicly: emphatically pro-Israel on scriptural grounds. Privately, in authenticated texts sent about 48 hours before his death: enraged at “bullying” by pro-Israel donors and threatening to leave “the pro-Israel cause.”

TPUSA’s spokesman, confirming the texts, called Kirk’s position “a wrestle that was going on for months.” That characterization, made against the organization’s public-relations interest, is the most evidence-consistent single-sentence verdict available.

Candace Owens and Tucker Carlson are the dominant sources for claims about Kirk’s private beliefs. Their records are not interchangeable. Owens’s document-backed claims have held up; her evidence-free claims have not. Carlson’s externally checkable claims have largely been corroborated, but every private view he attributes to Kirk matches Carlson’s own platform positions, and none is yet backed by a produced document.

Two further findings. First, the posthumous information environment is saturated with fabricated, misdated, and context-stripped quotes. Second, no evidence connects Kirk’s assassination to Israel or to any donor dispute. The criminal case against Tyler Robinson rests on a motive theory about Kirk’s views on gender and what Robinson called his “hate.” Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has denounced Israel-involvement theories as fabrications. The dispute documented here is about what Kirk believed, not about who killed him.

## A Note on Sources, Method, and the Misinformation Problem

Every substantive claim traces to identifiable reporting or primary material: Kirk’s broadcast statements, his letter to Netanyahu (published in full by the New York Post and cited by Bill Ackman and the Netanyahu government), his final WhatsApp messages (released by Candace Owens and authenticated by TPUSA spokesman Andrew Kolvet and thread participant Josh Hammer), contemporaneous coverage across outlets, and court reporting on the Robinson proceedings.

 Three cautions. Fabrication is pervasive after the assassination — viral graphics invented or distorted Kirk in both directions. Misdating is a distinct tactic: the October 2023 “stand-down” remarks later circulated as if they were recent. Interested parties dominate the private-views record. This report privileges authenticated documents, statements against interest, and claims corroborated by adversarial parties.

Grades used below: Verified recording, transcript, or authenticated document · Reliably reported / Contested named participants, disputed characterization · Unsupported no document, denied, or circular sourcing.

Part I

## 1. Foundations: Network-Level Zionism (2012–2018)

Kirk co-founded Turning Point USA in 2012, at eighteen, with donor Bill Montgomery. The organization was built inside pre-existing pro-Israel conservative infrastructure. In an April 2025 tribute to David Horowitz, Kirk wrote that TPUSA might not exist without him and that over ninety percent of the group’s earliest major donors were introduced at Horowitz events. Early and sustained funders included Home Depot co-founder Bernard Marcus; later, Robert Shillman became a seven-figure annual supporter — a relationship whose collapse in 2025 becomes central.

In this period Kirk’s Israel advocacy was standard-issue movement conservatism: Israel as the front line of Western civilization, the region’s democracy, a model of border security, and an obligation of the “Judeo-Christian” alliance. There is no serious dispute about this era. The commitment was, at this stage, primarily political and institutional. The theological register came later.

## 2. The Groyper Wars: Attacked From the Right for Israel (2019–2020)

For years, Kirk’s support for Israel was the thing his enemies on the far right hated him for. In fall 2019, Nick Fuentes launched the “Groyper Wars,” sending followers to pack question lines at TPUSA’s Culture War campus tour. At an Ohio State event on October 29, 2019, eleven of fourteen audience questions came from Groypers. The questions framed Kirk as loyal to a foreign country and leaned on recognized antisemitic dog whistles: the USS Liberty, “dancing Israelis,” dual loyalty.

Kirk denounced the campaign as racist and extremist, banned Fuentes from TPUSA events, and leaned further into his pro-Israel identity. During a March 2019 visit to Jerusalem at the invitation of Im Tirtzu, he told the audience he was very pro-Israel and that “my whole life I have defended Israel.” The Groypers spent the next half-decade calling him a “Zionist shill.” That history is why early speculation after the killing pointed, without evidence, at the Groypers — and why the later claim that Kirk had become a Fuentes-adjacent Israel critic strikes many who knew him as absurd.

## 3. Faith Deepens the Bond (2021–October 2023)

Around 2020–2021 Kirk moved from a largely secular activist posture to explicit Christian nationalism. Allies cited Trump’s 2018 embassy move to Jerusalem among the events that catalyzed that turn. In 2021 he launched TPUSA Faith with pastor Rob McCoy. Israel advocacy acquired a theological register: scriptural land rights, prophecy, the Holy Land as the place that made the Bible real to him. By December 2023, per Rabbi Pesach Wolicki, he had adopted weekly Sabbath observance. In his final year he described himself as learning biblical Hebrew and writing a book about the Sabbath.

Analytically this matters: by the mid-2020s, Kirk’s attachment to Israel ran through his Christianity, which is why late-life criticisms were experienced — and described by intimates — as a painful “wrestle” rather than a clean political repositioning.

## 4. October 7 and the First Cracks (October–December 2023)

After Hamas’s October 7 attack, Kirk defended Israel forcefully in public. The same window produced the first two verified departures from script.

### The “stand-down” question

On the Patrick Bet-David podcast on October 12, 2023, Kirk asked how the attack could have run for six hours in a country the size of New Jersey: “Was there a stand-down order?” He framed it as a legitimate question and declined to claim Netanyahu had foreknowledge. After his death the clip recirculated, misdated, as evidence of a recent break.

### The church strike statement (October 23, 2023)

After an Israeli strike partially destroyed buildings at the Greek Orthodox Church of Saint Porphyrius in Gaza City — a historic church compound sheltering Gaza’s Christian community, with a reported death toll near eighteen — Kirk posted that he was “disappointed by Israel’s response.” He demanded the intelligence justification, contrition, and a full accounting; noted Israel had quickly apologized for a mistaken strike on an Egyptian position; and observed that there had been more outrage over the misattributed al-Ahli hospital blast than over Christians dying at Gaza’s oldest church. Owens amplified him within hours. Both later described the backlash as formative. Kirk’s own post called the church “12th-century”; popular descriptions of the site run much older.

### The “Jewish donors” broadcasts

On October 26, 2023, Kirk said “Jewish donors have a lot of explaining to do,” calling them the number-one funding mechanism of radical, open-border, quasi-Marxist policies — a “beast” created by secular Jews that was now turning on Jews. On November 7 he called Jews among the “largest funders of cultural Marxist ideas” and argued Israel would remain in jeopardy so long as Western students were taught oppressor-oppressed ideology subsidized “with primarily Jewish dollars.” Defending Elon Musk later in November, he asserted that the philosophical foundation of “anti-whiteness” had been largely financed by Jewish donors and attacked the ADL.

Fact-checkers and Jewish organizations noted that “cultural Marxism” functions historically as an antisemitic conspiracy trope, and that claims about Jewish control of universities, nonprofits, and Hollywood echo classic stereotypes. Kirk’s contemporaneous defense: his target was liberal and secular Jewish philanthropy funding institutions hostile to both Israel and the West; his intent was protective; calling him antisemitic cheapened the word. Both readings work from the same verified transcripts. Note the 2023 target: left-leaning Jewish donors. By 2025 his complaints aimed at pro-Israel donors — a different group, indicted in similar vocabulary.

## 5. The Coalition Manager (2024)

Through 2024 the dominant fact of Kirk’s professional life on this issue was generational. Support for Israel eroded among young Americans, including young evangelicals and Gen Z Republicans — his constituency. On campus tours, by his team’s account, nearly half of audience questions were hostile challenges about Israel. Semafor’s post-assassination reporting described his number-one preoccupation as preventing the Israel question from ripping the MAGA coalition apart.

His method was accommodation plus argument: keep defending Israel on the merits, refuse to excommunicate the critics, platform them, and adopt some of their frames. He questioned unconditional aid in America First terms; entertained, without endorsing as fact, the theory that Jeffrey Epstein worked for Israeli intelligence; argued that reflexively calling young skeptics antisemites was false and strategically suicidal. Allies such as Ted Cruz and Dennis Prager saw a stalwart friend managing a rebellious flank. Critics of Israel read the same behavior as confusion. Semafor sources described him as personally “extraordinarily pro-Israel” but under pull from the online right around Tucker Carlson and JD Vance.

## 6. The Pressure Year (January–September 2025)

May 2. Private letter to Netanyahu — devotion, then a “5 alarm fire” warning that Israel was losing young conservatives and the information war. Analyzed in full in section 9.

July 11–13, Student Action Summit, Tampa. Kirk hosted Carlson, who used the stage to push an Epstein–Israeli-intelligence theory, and scheduled a formal Israel debate between Dave Smith and Josh Hammer. On stage Kirk condemned circulating Jew-hatred as brain-rotting and “demonic.” After a DOJ Epstein memo enraged the base and Trump called him, Kirk announced he was done talking about Epstein — then spent the next day walking that back to “that day.”

July, Epstein in Kirk’s own voice. As early as the October 2023 Bet-David interview he grouped Epstein with stories where “our guts are usually right.” In 2025 he lobbied for full file release. His most-cited formulation: the evidence showed Epstein was “a creation of” Mossad, Israeli intelligence, American intelligence, Saudi intelligence, or a hired gun. Fact-checkers have stressed the list-as-hypothesis framing. He never declared Epstein a Mossad agent in categorical terms. Israeli officials, including Naftali Bennett, denied any tie.

July 17, Holy Family Church. An Israeli tank shell hit Gaza’s only Catholic church, killing three and wounding the parish priest. Trump conveyed displeasure in some contemporaneous accounts; Netanyahu expressed regret over stray ammunition; an IDF probe found a misfired munition; Pope Leo XIV renewed a ceasefire call. No verified Kirk statement on this specific strike has surfaced. The claim that it affected him privately rests on posthumous testimony.

July–August, donor rupture. The New York Times later reported that Robert Shillman, angered by Carlson’s TPUSA appearance, canceled a two-million-dollar annual donation. Kirk’s subsequent private texts reference losing a major Jewish donor at exactly that sum for refusing to drop Carlson from December’s AmericaFest.

Early August, Bridgehampton. Kirk attended a Hamptons gathering with Bill Ackman and young conservatives. Ackman says Kirk proposed convening them after a cordial Zoom, and that both agreed treating all criticism of Israel as antisemitism is wrong. Attendee Seth Dillon corroborated the cordial account while confirming Kirk said people did not feel free to voice criticism of Israel. Owens describes the same event as a pressure “intervention,” with a Netanyahu call and money attached. Ackman, Kolvet, and Dillon dispute that characterization.

Summer focus group. In reported footage of a private discussion with young conservatives, Kirk aired aid skepticism, “decoupling,” AIPAC criticism, and antisemitism-label fatigue, and said “I’m trying to find this new path” alongside professions of love for Israel. Sourcing runs through advocacy outlets and TPUSA releases — a step below the letter and texts.

August, Megyn Kelly interview. Kirk described a “bulletproof” résumé of defending Israel, affirmed scriptural land rights and prophecy, expressed resentment at antisemitism accusations while learning Hebrew and writing a Sabbath book, and said the private and public behavior of “a lot of” the pro-Israel community was “pushing people like you and me away.”

c. September 8, final texts. In a WhatsApp group including Jewish associates, Kirk vented that his Jewish donors were “playing into all the stereotypes,” that pressure was “leaving me no choice but to leave the pro-Israel cause,” and: “I cannot and will not be bullied like this.” Authenticity is not in dispute.

September 9, last night. Zoom with two staunchly pro-Israel advisers, including Rabbi Wolicki, prepping answers to Israel questions dominating the tour. He had assigned staffer Blake Neff to master Benny Morris’s Righteous Victims. Kirk died still doing the work of defending Israel to his own audience.

## 7. Assassination and Immediate Aftermath (September 2025)

Kirk was shot and killed on September 10, 2025, while taking questions at Utah Valley University — first stop of the American Comeback Tour. The fatal exchange concerned mass shootings and transgender shooters, not Israel. Conspiracy theories alleging Israeli involvement flooded social media within hours, amplified by Fuentes, by Carlson’s insinuations about Kirk’s relationship with Netanyahu, and by Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene. Netanyahu called Kirk “an extraordinary friend,” quoted the letter’s devotional opening, and denounced Israel-involvement claims as a fabricated lie, invoking Goebbels.

Tyler Robinson, 22, was taken into custody within about 33 hours. Charging documents attribute statements that Kirk spread too much hate, and a note and texts to his romantic partner — a transgender roommate — saying he had “had enough of his hatred.” Nothing in the state’s case involves Israel, donors, or foreign actors. Kirk received a posthumous Presidential Medal of Freedom on October 14, 2025. The Israeli city of Netanya renamed a traffic circle for him. His September 21 memorial drew tens of thousands at State Farm Stadium (contemporary estimates generally 70,000–90,000, not a full hundred thousand).

In late September, conservatives erupted over the ADL’s “Glossary of Extremism and Hate” entry on TPUSA. The ADL edited its TPUSA backgrounder, then retired the thousand-entry glossary on September 30; the FBI severed its relationship with the ADL a day later.

## 8. The Battle Over His Legacy (October 2025–July 2026)

In late September the New York Post published the Netanyahu letter in full after Owens accused Netanyahu of quoting only its opening. On October 6–7, Owens released the final texts; on October 8, Kolvet authenticated them, said he had given them to the FBI, and described a months-long “wrestle.” Hammer insisted the texts were sarcastic venting about a lost donation. Owens escalated to insinuating foreknowledge by thread members — a claim with no evidentiary support.

Through October and November the right’s Israel fight became open warfare over Kirk’s ghost. Carlson’s late-October interview with Fuentes triggered a movement crisis. The New York Times published Michelle Goldberg’s column on Fuentes as Kirk’s successor. Ted Cruz publicly blasted Carlson’s circle for claiming Kirk’s posthumous agreement.

AmericaFest, December 18–21, 2025 — record attendance of more than 30,000, led by new CEO Erika Kirk — became the fight’s public stage. Kirk had refused to cancel Carlson from this event at a reported cost of two million dollars a year. Shapiro opened by attacking Owens, Kelly, and Carlson; Bannon derided the “Israel first crowd.” The event remained institutionally pro-Israel; Vance closed with a unity message. The question Kirk spent his last years managing had outlived him unmanaged.

As of the report’s July 8, 2026 freeze date, Robinson’s preliminary hearing was underway in Provo. Prosecutors presented surveillance, DNA, a confession note, and a motive theory grounded in Robinson’s hostility to Kirk’s views on gender. Nothing in the proceedings involves Israel. (Testimony later wrapped July 10, with arguments set for September 1 — after this report’s date.)

Part II

## 9. The Letter to Netanyahu (May 2, 2025)

The single most probative document for Kirk’s state of mind in his final year. Sent privately four months before his death; existence disclosed by Netanyahu; published in full by the New York Post; Ackman said its sentiments matched what Kirk told him weeks later.

 Devotion as premise. Advocacy for Israel and alliance with Jews in defense of Judeo-Christian civilization as one of his greatest joys as a Christian. Everything in the letter, he wrote, comes from deep love — and real love requires brutal honesty.

 Diagnosis. Anti-Israel and antisemitic content at record levels. A catalog of hostile campus questions. His double bind: branded a paid apologist when he defends Israel, branded antisemitic when he defends insufficiently. “Israel is losing support even in conservative circles. This should be a 5 alarm fire.”

 Prescription. Run Israel’s image like a political campaign: original content, fight in the first person, spend hasbara budget on the actual digital battlefield, visible reciprocity such as Israeli aid after an American hurricane.

 Conclusion. Netanyahu is “losing the information war,” which will translate into reduced American support. Watching support slip away pains him because the Holy Land is central to his life.

Read whole, the letter is neither a break nor uncomplicated cheerleading. It is an inside critic’s memo. Both posthumous factions quote it accurately and partially.

## 10. The Final Text Messages (c. September 8, 2025)

Authenticated content, in summary: a major Jewish donor had pulled two million dollars a year over refusal to cancel Carlson from AmericaFest; “Jewish donors” were “playing into all the stereotypes”; pressure was “leaving me no choice but to leave the pro-Israel cause”; “I cannot and will not be bullied like this.” He floated inviting Owens.

Three readings. Venting (Hammer, substantially Kolvet): furious, partly sarcastic blow-off about a specific donor dispute from a man whose same-week work was still pro-Israel; “leave the cause” means leave the enforcement apparatus. Genuine breaking point (Owens, Carlson, substantially Kelly): the texts state a conclusion sent to people who would relay it. Unstable middle (this report): anger and threatened exit were real; the object was the donor-enforcement system, not Jews or the state of Israel; the “stereotypes” reflex is the same trope-adjacent move documented in 2023.

A caution against the conspiracy reading: the texts were about money and autonomy, they name no one, they were handed to the FBI by TPUSA, and the criminal case contains no connective tissue to any donor or to Israel.

## 12. The Owens Testimony

Owens is among the best-positioned witnesses and among the least reliable narrators in American media. Neither fact cancels the other. Method: grade each claim on its own corroboration.

Governing pattern. Where she produced documents — the final texts — adversarial verification confirmed them. Where she asserted without documents — intervention framing, money offers, fear-for-his-life, assassination theories — named witnesses disputed her, and several claims were formally rebutted.

Moving toward Catholicism. Owens claimed on September 11, 2025, that Kirk had been praying the rosary and attending Mass, and that Mary was the sticking point. She tied the religious claim to geopolitics: drift toward Rome and drift from Israel-centric theology as one process. John Yep of Catholics for Catholics has said Kirk privately disclosed movement toward conversion. Rebuttal: TPUSA Faith director Lucas Miles stated in March 2026 that Kirk never entered OCIA, never had his marriage convalidated, remained evangelical, and could not reconcile Mariology. Grade: respect for Catholicism verified; conversion trajectory unsupported by any document; funeral-Mass claim unverified.

Church strikes. 2023 St. Porphyrius element verified. Extension to the July 2025 Holy Family strike is plausible and uncorroborated.

Hamptons “intervention,” Netanyahu call, money. Kernel corroborated (gathering happened; Israel discussed; Netanyahu did invite Kirk to Israel; Dillon confirms the “not free to voice” line). Characterization contested. Financial claims unsupported.

Netanyahu offered to fund TPUSA. Most consequential unverified claim in the record. Genealogy: Grayzone anonymous source (Sept. 12) → Owens “ton of money” (Sept. 15) → Fuentes $150 million citing Owens and Blumenthal. Kolvet, named by Owens as a source, denied in July 2026 that any offer to Kirk, TPUSA, or the show existed. Owens conceded the $150 million figure never came from him. No document. Verified money pressure ran the opposite direction — withdrawal, not inducement. Grade: unsupported.

Feared for his life; assassination theories. Sole-source or evidence-free. The state’s case contains nothing connecting to Israel, donors, or foreign actors. Trump rejected the French-squad claim in April 2026.

Honest use of Owens: as a pointer to documents, never as a substitute for them.

## 13. The Carlson Testimony

Standing is strong and current: Kirk platformed him at SAS over donor objections, absorbed a reported two-million-dollar loss rather than drop him, and pledged days before death to lock him in for AmericaFest. Reliability differs from Owens: Carlson insinuates more than he asserts, and his externally checkable factual claims have tended to check out. What he shares with Owens is alignment of interest. Every private conviction he attributes to Kirk doubles as validation of Carlson’s platform.

Governing pattern. External claims (donor punishment, $2 million loss, resentment of pressure) are substantially corroborated. Internal claims (what Kirk privately believed) are attributed, undocumented, and isomorphic to Carlson’s views.

Privately despised Netanyahu / appalled by Gaza. Carlson, September 17, 2025: Kirk told him “many times” Netanyahu was “a very destructive force,” was appalled by Gaza, and resented the U.S. being used to prosecute Israel’s wars. Carlson said texts exist. None produced. Gaetz vouched; Cruz disputed. Directionally compatible with the letter’s criticisms; harsher than anything in Kirk’s verified record. Grade: attributed, unverified, contested.

Donors “tormented” him. Substance verified; “tormented” is characterization.

“Go all the way” at SAS. Encouragement to platform Carlson is consistent with Kolvet and with Kirk’s own Epstein hypothesis. The quoted exchange is single-witness.

Memorial speech, September 21, 2025. Verified. Jesus in Jerusalem, truth-telling, lamp-lit room, men “eating hummus,” “why don’t we just kill him.” Critics heard the deicide trope; far-right admirers heard the same thing. The implied claim that Jews or Israel killed Kirk is supported by nothing and contradicted by the criminal record.

Honest use of Carlson: accept facts documents confirm; treat attributed convictions as one interested friend’s account, entitled to the same weight as Cruz’s, Wolicki’s, and Prager’s opposite ones.

Part III

## 14. What Changed and What Did Not

Constant: public identification as pro-Israel; theological attachment that deepened rather than weakened; categorical public condemnation of antisemitism, including in his final summer; refusal to join the Fuentes right. No verified statement expresses hostility to Israel’s existence or to Jews as such.

What changed: (1) basis of support migrated from network/political to theological, 2020–2022; (2) willingness to indict “Jewish money” as a causal force, in trope-structured language, provided the frame was protective, fall 2023; (3) posture shifted from advocate to referee, 2024–2025; (4) relationship with the pro-Israel enforcement apparatus broke down completely, in private, at the end. Endpoint at death: unresolved — prepping Israel’s defense for the next morning and privately threatening to quit the cause’s organized wing.

## 15. The Two “Jewish Donors” Complaints

Kirk complained about “Jewish donors” twice, three years apart, about different people. The complaints are routinely merged. In 2023 the target was liberal and secular Jewish philanthropy. In 2025 the target was pro-Israel conservative donors — his own funders — blamed for coercing editorial choices. What unites the episodes is the reflex: under pressure, Kirk collapsed a specific dispute with specific wealthy individuals into a generalization about Jewish donors as a class. Defenders note he was describing real, reported behavior by actual donors, that the second complaint was private, and that generalizing about donor classes is ordinary political speech when the class is not Jewish. Both “he used antisemitic tropes” and “he was a lifelong friend of the Jews” survive contact with the evidence because of this double structure.

## 17. Drivers of the Shift

 Audience. Gen Z conservatism was souring on Israel; half his Q&A had become interrogation. The Netanyahu letter is battlefield reporting from that front.

 Friends. Carlson and Vance exerted documented pull. Kirk disagreed with Carlson on Israel yet defied donors to platform him twice.

 Faith. Anchored the attachment and, per Carlson, fed revulsion at human costs in Gaza.

 The war’s length, imagery, and churches. Kirk’s sharpest verified public criticism of Israel (October 2023) came in response to a church strike. The July 2025 Holy Family strike landed in the window of final-summer turmoil.

 Enforcement backlash. By his own texts, donor ultimatums were the proximate cause of the threatened exit. Even sympathetic pro-Israel commentary warned that purity-testing Kirk was self-defeating.
