# The Surrender of Tyler Robinson: Timeline, Details, and Sourcing
*State of Utah v. Tyler James Robinson — research memo, compiled July 15, 2026 (v1.1, audited)*
*All times Mountain Time unless noted. Allegations from charging documents are the prosecution's account, not adjudicated fact.*

---

## 1. Summary

Tyler Robinson was not identified by investigators. Sworn testimony at the July 2026 preliminary hearing confirmed that law enforcement did not know the shooter's identity until Robinson surrendered; the surveillance footage alone was insufficient to identify him. The chain, per charging documents and official statements: Robinson's mother recognized his likeness in FBI-released person-of-interest photos on Sept. 11, 2025; his father agreed and also fixed on the publicized rifle description; the parents drew him to their home in Washington, Utah, where he allegedly implied he was the shooter and expressed suicidal intent; the parents brought in a family friend — a retired Washington County Sheriff's Office (WCSO) detective — who called Sheriff Nate Brooksby at 8:02 p.m.; Robinson arrived at the WCSO office (Hurricane, Utah) just before 9 p.m. with his parents and the friend, and was transported to Utah County overnight.

The widely repeated claim that "his father turned him in" originates chiefly with President Trump's Sept. 12 Fox & Friends remarks and anonymous-source reporting. The official account (Gov. Cox, Sheriff Brooksby, charging documents) describes a negotiated voluntary surrender facilitated by the family collectively and the retired-detective intermediary — not a unilateral report by the father to authorities. Several timing conflicts in the record remain unresolved (Section 5).

## 2. Timeline

### Wednesday, Sept. 10, 2025
- **~12:20 p.m.** — Kirk is shot at UVU. (AP timeline)
- **4:21 p.m.** — FBI Director Kash Patel posts that a subject is in custody. **4:30 p.m.** — officials say no one is in custody. **5:59 p.m.** — Patel posts that the subject was released after interrogation. (AP timeline; the detained individuals, including George Zinn, were cleared of the shooting itself.)
- **Evening (times not public)** — Per charging documents: Robinson allegedly texts his roommate/partner Lance Twiggs to look under his keyboard, where a handwritten note stating he had the opportunity to kill Kirk and intended to take it is found; an extended text exchange follows. (Charging docs via AP/ABC)

### Thursday, Sept. 11, 2025
- **Morning (exact clock time not published in reviewed sources)** — FBI Salt Lake City releases two grainy photos of a "person of interest" (black long-sleeve shirt, sunglasses, hat); a separate post "late Thursday morning" announces a reward of up to $100,000. (FBI SLC posts via Nexstar's contemporaneous Sept. 11 report; note the FBI's own updates page timestamps only the *evening* releases, so the morning times rest on media sequencing.)
- **Timing not specified in filings** — Per charging documents (as reported by CNN and the Salt Lake Tribune): Robinson's mother sees the released images and calls her son; he says he has been home sick since the previous day. She shows her husband, who agrees on the resemblance. The father also notes the distinctive rifle publicized by investigators and asks his son to send a photo of his rifle. Charging documents quote a Robinson text to Twiggs saying his dad wanted rifle photos and was calling him. Robinson's father eventually reaches him by phone; Robinson allegedly implies he intends to kill himself. His parents persuade him to come to their home in Washington, Utah. There, per the charging documents, Robinson implies he was the shooter and says he couldn't go to jail and wanted to end it. In a phone conversation with his father, per the documents, he allegedly explained the act by saying Kirk spread too much hate/evil. Utah County Attorney Jeff Gray declined to characterize these statements as a confession, saying a judge would determine that.
- **Afternoon/evening** — The parents involve a longtime family friend: a retired WCSO detective, connected to the family through religious association, whom hearing testimony later identified as **"Mitchell"** (ABC4's account of Davis's testimony does not specify whether this is a given name or surname; no fuller name published) and described as one of Robinson's former Boy Scout leaders. Per Brooksby (Fox13 account), during the friend's call it was relayed that Robinson had expressed suicidal ideation and had been en route to a remote part of Washington County before his parents talked him out of it and pledged to stand by him through a peaceful surrender.
- **7:05 p.m. (9:05 p.m. ET)** — FBI releases four additional, clearer photos of the person of interest. (FBI.gov updates page)
- **~7:57 p.m.** — A Discord message from Robinson's account tells groupmates it was him at UVU and that he is surrendering through a sheriff friend momentarily. (Message text attributed to Washington Post reporting by secondary accounts — first-publication claim not independently verified here. The ~7:57 p.m. timestamp is per The American Prospect; a screenshot attributed to a TPUSA spokesman circulates via Tier-4 aggregation. One aggregator renders it 8:57 p.m. — likely a timezone artifact. See Section 5.)
- **~8:00 p.m. (10 p.m. ET)** — FBI releases video of the shooter jumping from the Losee Center roof; Cox and Patel hold an evening press conference pleading for public help; 7,000+ tips reported. (FBI.gov; CNN live coverage)
- **8:02 p.m.** — Brooksby receives the call from the retired detective, who says he knows the shooter's identity, knows the family through religious association, and is working to bring him in voluntarily. Brooksby immediately calls Utah County Sheriff Mike Smith. (Brooksby, Sept. 17 press conference, via CNN/AP)
- **Just before 9 p.m.** — Robinson arrives at the WCSO office in Hurricane with his parents and the family friend; he is met by plainclothes detectives (CNN), consistent with the negotiated "gentle" surrender conditions Brooksby described (Fox13). WCSO does not question him; he sits unrestrained on a couch with his parents, offered water, while state and federal investigators travel south. Deputies secure the parents' home and Robinson's St. George apartment. (Brooksby, Sept. 17 presser, via CNN, Police1, Fox13)
- **~10 p.m.** — The publicly cited custody time. An inmate booking sheet (obtained and posted publicly by a third party; cited by The American Prospect) lists arrest at 10:00 p.m. on Sept. 11 by SBI agent Brian Davis, location outside Utah County.

### Friday, Sept. 12, 2025
- **Overnight transport** — Brooksby: Robinson was driven to Utah County by about 2 a.m. The booking sheet lists booking into Utah County Jail at **1:58 a.m.** Davis's sworn testimony (July 8, 2026) was reported by ABC4 as booking **around 7 a.m.**, and by NewsNation as a formal arrest **around 4 a.m.** These accounts conflict; see Section 5.
- **Processing** — Phone seized; DNA buccal swab, fingerprints, and handprints collected under warrant. Davis interviewed the mother; other investigators interviewed Robinson and his father; the family friend was also interviewed. (Davis testimony, July 8, 2026, via ABC4/CBS/NewsNation)
- **Morning (exact time not established in reviewed sources)** — Trump, on Fox & Friends (airs 6–9 a.m. ET, i.e., 4–7 a.m. MT), says he believes with high certainty the suspect is in custody — necessarily ahead of the 8:05 a.m. MT official announcement if said live on air — and gives his version of the surrender (see Section 4).
- **8:05 a.m.** — Authorities announce Robinson is in custody (AP timeline). At a Friday-morning press conference, Cox announces "we got him" (CNN); The American Prospect describes Patel confirming the arrest at a *mid-morning* press conference. Reviewed sources do not establish whether the 8:05 a.m. announcement and the press conference were the same event.
- **~10:20 a.m.** — Probable cause statement outlines arrest on suspicion of aggravated murder, felony discharge, and obstruction. (AP timeline)

### Subsequent record
- **Sept. 16, 2025** — Formal charges filed (aggravated murder, felony discharge, obstruction, witness tampering, violent offense in presence of a child); death penalty noticed. Charging documents lay out the surrender sequence above.
- **Sept. 17, 2025** — Brooksby press conference: details the 33-hour manhunt framing and the surrender logistics; issues two corrections — (a) the intermediary was a retired WCSO detective, **not** a youth pastor or church leader; (b) the viral claim that Robinson's father is a current deputy who personally handcuffed his son is false. The father shares the name **Matt Robinson** with an unrelated retired WCSO corrections deputy, who was harassed over the confusion. (Police1; St. George News)
- **March 27–30, 2026** — Brooksby resigns/retires after complaints to county commissioners about sheriff's office operations; subsequent reporting cites sexual harassment allegations and internal-handling questions; the Utah AG's office declined a criminal investigation; the county paid $100,000 severance per April reporting based on county statements (SLTrib, Apr. 3; St. George News, Apr. 1) — note a March 30 county-attorney comment had initially downplayed any severance beyond already-approved salary (KUTV), a within-record evolution. **No public evidence connects the resignation to the Robinson case**, though online commentary has speculated. (SLTrib; St. George News; KUTV)
- **March 30, 2026** — Defense files a 258-page motion. Per The American Prospect, page 70 contains a transcript labeled as from the evening of his arrest in which an officer reads Robinson Miranda rights at **6:25–6:26 p.m.** and Robinson asks to first speak with attorney Doug Terry, whom his parents were trying to retain. The defense itself did not flag any timeline discrepancy from this. (See Section 5.)
- **Late March 2026** — Mitchell provides a written statement to the Utah County Attorney's Office; admitted into evidence at the preliminary hearing July 8, 2026, but not displayed in court. (Davis testimony via ABC4)
- **July 7, 2026 (prelim Day 2)** — On cross, agent David Hull acknowledges investigators did not know the shooter's identity until Robinson turned himself in and that surveillance footage alone could not identify him; confirms the facilitator was known to the family — the defense's question framed him as personally known to Robinson and connected to law enforcement, and Hull said he had become aware the man had been one of Robinson's Boy Scout leaders. (St. George News; Post Millennial)
- **July 8, 2026 (prelim Day 3)** — Davis testifies to the surrender/processing sequence; prosecutors play a short, audio-less WCSO video of Robinson standing in a room in a maroon/burgundy T-shirt and dark cap — clothing matching the campus surveillance of the suspect earlier that day, a point the state emphasized. (ABC4; CBS; TMZ; Fox4)

## 3. The intermediary ("family friend")

| Attribute | Best-sourced account |
|---|---|
| Role | Retired WCSO detective (Brooksby, Sept. 17); "retired deputy" in AP timeline |
| Name | "Mitchell" (Davis testimony, July 8, 2026, via ABC4 — unspecified whether given name or surname); no fuller name published in reviewed reporting |
| Relationship | Known to family through religious association (Brooksby); former Boy Scout leader of Robinson's (Hull testimony, July 7, 2026) |
| Not | A youth pastor or church leader (Brooksby's explicit correction, Sept. 17) |
| Actions | Called Brooksby directly at 8:02 p.m. Sept. 11; helped persuade Robinson; accompanied him and his parents to the WCSO office; interviewed by investigators; gave written statement to prosecutors (March 2026), admitted at prelim |

Early conflicting descriptions: Trump described a minister/man of faith connected to law enforcement who went to a U.S. Marshal; CNN, citing an unnamed law-enforcement source (Sept. 13), described a youth pastor working with WCSO and the U.S. Marshals Service; some secondary outlets said the friend called the U.S. Marshals. Brooksby's on-record account — a direct call to him from a retired WCSO detective — supersedes these under this project's sourcing hierarchy, and Brooksby explicitly rebutted the youth-pastor characterization.

## 4. The father's role: claim vs. record

**Popular framing:** "His father turned him in." Principal origin: Trump on Fox & Friends, Sept. 12 (someone close to him turned him in; the father convinced the son; a minister/U.S. Marshal involved), plus Fox News sourcing that the father "played a role."

**Official record:** Cox (Sept. 12): a family member reached out to a family friend, who contacted WCSO with information that Robinson had confessed to or implied involvement to them. Charging documents (Sept. 16): both parents recognized him; the father pressed on the rifle; both parents persuaded him against suicide and toward surrender; the friend arranged it. Brooksby: the tip call came from the retired detective, not the father; the father did not handcuff or deliver his son in any law-enforcement capacity.

**Assessment:** The father was central — recognition, the rifle-photo request, the persuasion — but "the father called the police on his son" is not what the official record describes. The report to law enforcement came through the intermediary, with the family's cooperation, as a negotiated voluntary surrender. Both parents accompanied Robinson to the sheriff's office.

## 5. Discrepancies and open questions (flagged, not resolved)

1. **Miranda at 6:25 p.m. vs. the surrender chronology.** The defense-motion transcript (per The American Prospect, April 15, 2026) records Miranda at 6:25–6:26 p.m. on "the evening of his arrest," with Robinson invoking counsel. If that evening was Sept. 11, it precedes the 7:57 p.m. Discord post, the 8:02 p.m. Brooksby call, the ~9 p.m. arrival, and the 10 p.m. booking-sheet arrest time — implying custody hours earlier than every official account. The transcript's date has not been independently anchored in open court in reviewed reporting; the defense did not itself raise a discrepancy and, in the same brief, criticized circulation of an "electronic fake confession" (an apparent reference to the Discord post) without connecting it to the timestamp. Lead counsel Kathryn Nester did not respond to the Prospect's inquiries. Mundane explanations have not been ruled in or out publicly — most importantly, because the arrest process spanned midnight (booking-sheet arrest 10 p.m. Sept. 11; booking and formal processing Sept. 12), the label "the evening of his arrest" is itself date-ambiguous: read as Sept. 12, the transcript could describe a 6:25 p.m. interview attempt at the Utah County Jail and no conflict exists; read as Sept. 11, it is irreconcilable with the official chronology. Reviewed reporting does not resolve which date the underlying document bears. Status: **unresolved; primary document not publicly authenticated in reviewed reporting.**
2. **Arrest/booking times conflict across the record.** Booking sheet: arrested 10 p.m. Sept. 11, booked 1:58 a.m. Sept. 12. Brooksby (via CNN paraphrase): driven to Utah County "by about 2 a.m." — ambiguous between departure and arrival, though arrival-by-2 is consistent with a 1:58 a.m. booking. Davis's sworn testimony as reported: formally arrested ~4 a.m. (NewsNation) / booked ~7 a.m. (ABC4). Strictly, the direct contradiction is 1:58 a.m. vs. ~7 a.m. for the *same field* (booking time); the 10 p.m. vs. ~4 a.m. arrest gap could in principle reflect custody-vs-formal-arrest distinctions, but a 4 a.m. formal arrest *after* a 1:58 a.m. booking would be anomalous. Caveat: the hearing figures are reporters' paraphrases of testimony, not certified transcript, so some of this conflict may be reporting error rather than record error. The roughly 3–3.5-hour drive from Hurricane to the Utah County Jail (Spanish Fork) constrains any reconstruction. Resolution path: certified Day 3 transcript (Fourth District Court clerk) plus the booking sheet via GRAMA. Status: **conflicting; unreconciled in public reporting.**
3. **The WCSO surrender video is short, audio-less, and (per available reporting) not publicly timestamped**, limiting its value for anchoring the chronology. (Fox4; CBS)
4. **Discord timestamp rendering:** ~7:57 p.m. MT (Prospect/TPUSA spokesman screenshot) vs. "8:57 p.m." in one aggregator — likely timezone conversion error, but the underlying platform-native timestamp has not been published in a verified form.
5. **When the mother recognized the photos:** CNN's charging-document account implies the morning release; the AP timeline places the recognition entry after its 7:55 p.m. evening photo/video entry. Filings reviewed via reporting do not give clock times for the family interactions. (Related minor variance: AP lists the evening release at 7:55 p.m.; the FBI's own page timestamps the four-photo release at 9:05 p.m. ET = 7:05 p.m. MT and the video at 10 p.m. ET = 8:00 p.m. MT — AP's time may reflect a separate Utah DPS post.)
6. **Youth pastor / U.S. Marshals versions** (Trump; CNN's unnamed source; secondary outlets) vs. Brooksby's direct on-record correction. Status: **superseded by Tier-2 official statement**, but the early versions still circulate.
7. **Surrender location wording:** some outlets say "St. George"; the WCSO office where he surrendered is in Hurricane, Utah.
8. **Brooksby's March 2026 resignation** is factually confirmed and relates to complaints about office operations/harassment allegations; **no reviewed source ties it evidentially to the Robinson case**, though it features heavily in speculative commentary.

## 7. Bottom line

The core surrender narrative — family recognition, family persuasion, retired-detective intermediary, negotiated peaceful surrender at WCSO the evening of Sept. 11 — is consistent across charging documents, Cox, Brooksby, and sworn 2026 testimony. Of these, only the investigators' account (Hull, Davis) has been tested under oath and on cross-examination; Cox's and Brooksby's versions remain untested Tier-2 press statements, and Brooksby is no longer in office. What is *not* settled: the precise custody clock (booking times directly conflict between the booking sheet and reported testimony), and the 6:25 p.m. Miranda transcript reported from the defense's own filing — which is irreconcilable with the official evening chronology only if the underlying document is dated Sept. 11, a fact reviewed reporting does not establish, and which has received no official explanation.
