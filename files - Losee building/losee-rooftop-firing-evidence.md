# Evidence That a Gun Was Fired From the Losee Center Rooftop
## State v. Tyler James Robinson — Assassination of Charlie Kirk (Sept. 10, 2025)

**Document scope:** This document summarizes, per the official narrative and sworn preliminary-hearing testimony (July 6–8, 2026, Fourth District Court, Provo, Utah), what physical evidence does and does not exist that a firearm was discharged on the rooftop of Utah Valley University's Losee Center — the location from which prosecutors allege Tyler Robinson fired the single fatal shot.

**Prepared:** July 9, 2026. Case status: preliminary hearing in progress; all claims are allegations pending trial. Robinson has pleaded not guilty.

---

## 1. Direct Firing Evidence on the Rooftop: None Recovered

- **No shell casings or bullets on or around the roof.** Lead case agent David Hull confirmed on cross-examination by defense attorney Kathryn Nester that no shell casings or bullets were found on or around the Losee Center roof [1]. (Hull was a State Bureau of Investigation Major Crimes Division investigator at the time of the shooting and is now a Utah Department of Public Safety / POST investigator; AP-syndicated coverage labels him a "former" SBI agent, but this reflects a role change, not retirement [10].)
- **No firearm visible in surveillance footage.** Hull acknowledged that investigators never actually saw a firearm in the rooftop video — only a long object he believed was consistent with a concealed rifle [1].
- **No gunshot residue (GSR) evidence from the rooftop has been disclosed.** No GSR testing of the rooftop surface has surfaced in public testimony, charging documents, or reported court filings. A separate, widely circulated claim — advanced by commentator Candace Owens — holds that Robinson himself was never given a standard GSR test [11]. That claim traces to commentary citing unnamed sourcing and is not confirmed by any official record; it should be treated as low-verification (Tier 4). Two forensic caveats apply regardless: GSR evidence is substantially less probative than popular expectations suggest, and primer residue is easily lost, wiped, or transferred — a suspect processed roughly a day after the event, in reportedly the same clothing, could plausibly test negative or positive for reasons unrelated to guilt [12]. Absence of GSR, even if confirmed, would carry limited evidentiary weight in either direction.

## 2. What Was Found on the Rooftop (Positional / Trace Evidence)

- **"Sniper pad" gravel impressions.** Former UVU officer Christopher Bagley testified he found disturbed gravel near the roof edge that looked like someone had lain prone — impressions consistent with a person's elbows, knees, and feet — with a direct line of sight to the stage where Kirk was speaking [2]. Bagley described it in court as looking "like a sniper pad" [1].
- **A shoe impression**, documented by officers before the roof was secured [2].
- **A screwdriver**, recovered from the roof and sent to the FBI for DNA analysis [4]. The precise testimony matters here: FBI analyst Amanda Bakker testified the testing did **not** identify Robinson as the source of the DNA, but found he **could not be excluded as a "possible contributor"** to the DNA recovered from the towel and screwdriver [5]. Prosecution summaries and some headlines compress this into "Robinson's DNA was found on it," which overstates the analyst's own words; "possible contributor" is mandatory U.S. Department of Justice reporting convention, not an evidentiary concession [13]. DNA from Robinson's roommate and partner, Lance Twiggs, was also present on both items, though Bakker testified Twiggs' sample was used only as an elimination sample; the defense challenged sample degradation and test accuracy [5][13].
- **Caveat surfaced on cross-examination:** Bagley did not personally see the body imprint on the roof; he learned of it as prosecutors prepared him to testify [3].

## 3. Auditory and Line-of-Sight Evidence

- Bagley testified he heard a single gunshot and maintained it did not sound like a handgun; it sounded like a rifle, with a deeper, "more violent" echo [3].
- From the outdoor amphitheater, Bagley observed that Kirk's white tent was directly in the Losee Center's line of sight [3].
- Rooftop surveillance clips, as narrated by Hull, show an individual climbing onto the roof, running across it, lying prone in a corner, then lowering himself off the roof and leaving while carrying something; Hull estimated the shooter was prone for roughly 15–30 seconds before firing [1][5].

## 4. The Fired-Casing Evidence Exists — But Was Recovered Off-Roof

- The alleged murder weapon — a Mauser Model 98 bolt-action rifle (.30-06) — was found wrapped in a towel in a wooded area near campus, containing **one spent round and three unspent rounds** [6].
- The ATF **identified the spent cartridge case as having been fired from that rifle** [7].
- Prosecutors say DNA consistent with Robinson's was found on the rifle's trigger, the fired cartridge casing, two other cartridges, and the towel [6]. The defense counters that forensic reports show DNA from multiple people — up to "five or more" — on some items, requiring more complex analysis [6].
- **Ballistic link to the fatal bullet remains inconclusive.** The ATF's light-comparison-microscopy analysis (report dated Sept. 17, 2025) could neither identify nor exclude the bullet jacket fragment recovered at Kirk's autopsy as having been fired from the rifle [7]. The FBI was reported to be conducting a second comparative bullet analysis and a bullet lead analysis [8]. Fact-checkers and firearms experts note "inconclusive" is a common examination outcome and is not an exoneration [8][9].

## 5. Analytical Note: Why the Absence of a Roof Casing Cuts Both Ways

The absence of a shell casing on the roof is consistent with the prosecution's own theory of the weapon: a bolt-action Mauser retains the spent case in the chamber unless the shooter cycles the bolt, and the recovered rifle did in fact contain a spent round still chambered [6]. On this account, no ejected casing would be expected on the roof.

At the same time, the state's case that the shot originated from the Losee Center roof rests entirely on **circumstantial evidence at that location** — gravel impressions, line of sight, video of a prone figure carrying an unidentified long object, a DNA-bearing screwdriver, and witness acoustics — rather than any direct physical evidence of a discharge there (casing, bullet, or disclosed GSR). The direct firing evidence (fired casing matched to the rifle) attaches to the weapon recovered in the woods, and its connection to the rooftop depends on the surveillance-video narrative and DNA chain, both of which the defense is actively contesting [1][4].

## Sourcing Methodology

All claims are drawn from mainstream news reporting of sworn preliminary-hearing testimony (July 6–8, 2026), published charging documents, and court filings quoted in that reporting. Testimony elicited on cross-examination is attributed as such. Claims by the prosecution and defense are labeled as allegations or arguments, not established fact. Where evidence has not been publicly disclosed (e.g., rooftop GSR testing), the document notes the absence of disclosure rather than asserting the evidence does not exist.
