## How to read this

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ID** | Stable handle. `Q-nn`. Never reused. |
| **P** | Priority 1 (do next) → 5 (someday) |
| **Status** | `ready` · `blocked` · `doing` · `done` · `parked` (needs a human decision) |
| **Blocked by** | Q-IDs, or an external condition |
| **Decisive artifact** | The specific thing that closes it. If this column is empty the item is not actionable — fix that before dispatching it. |
| **Cost** | `S` <1h · `M` a session · `L` multi-session · `XL` a programme |
| **Gate** | `auto` — a loop may execute and commit · `verify` — must survive an independent pass before findings harden · `human` — stop and ask |

**Gate rules are not advisory.** See LOOP.md §3. Anything touching **case posture**, **a living person's conduct**, or **spending money** is `human` regardless of what else it says.

## Standing hazards (read before dispatching anything)

1. **Selective quotation** — a quotation by an interested party is reliable as to the words, never as to the document. (CR-ACQ §0)
2. **Provenance** — a forensic report is silent about where its exhibits came from. Provenance is a **separate** verification act. (CR-AUDIT §4)
3. **Sampling** — keyword sampling of sworn testimony finds *topics* and misses *conclusions*. (CR-E02 §1)
4. **Metadata vs. content** — a page date is a fact about a publishing schedule, not about a courtroom. **Always ask what a datum is a fact *about*.** (D-51 reversal, CR-E03 §7)
5. **Search nulls** — a null from a text search is a claim about the search until the method is validated against a known positive. (BOC-AUDIT-2026-07-27B §4)
6. **Register numbering** — grep, then **read the hit**. ⚠ **Forward-looking "next is D-nn" notes matched the pattern five times without causing an error — then caused one on 2026-08-13:** CR-E07 read the prose D-59s as a prior high and assigned D-60, leaving a gap the string-counting validator could not see (corrected in place 2026-08-14, CR-E07 v1.2). **Reading the hit is not optional.** ⚠ Also: the grep has no word boundary, so **any string ending in "…D" followed by a hyphen and digits registers as a D-number** — a file named `…PROPOSED<hyphen>20260814.md` (written here with `<hyphen>` deliberately) broke the check the moment its name was quoted in a markdown file (renamed 2026-08-14). Mind what you name things.
7. **Exhibit numbers are not unique** — the State and the defence each have an "Exhibit 19" on Day 4, and the transcript renders both bare. **Always write "State's" or "the defence's."** Merging them would put the chamber cartridge case inside the AFTE proficiency-test demonstration. (CR-AUDIT-2026-07-28 §2.1)
10. **Transcript timestamps are RECORDING-STREAM time. They are LOCATORS.** ⚠ **CORRECTED 2026-08-01 (Q-54) — the previous wording of this hazard was itself wrong.** It said "the clock jumps across every recess (Day 2: ~1 hour)"; **it does not.** On **Days 1 and 2 the recording runs *through* every recess as dead air** — the Day 2 "~1 hour discontinuity" is the 73 min 54 s lunch recess, recorded, with a `(silence)` turn inside it — and two clock readings by the judge fix the same offset to within two seconds across an intervening recess. **So: a duration measured across a recess on Days 1–2 IS REAL** (this is why the "ten-minute" break on Day 1 genuinely ran twenty-two minutes). **What remains forbidden is converting a timestamp to a time of day and presenting it as one** — the reconstruction is good only to about **±5 minutes** and must be marked approximate. ⚠ **Days 3, 4 and 5 are UNTESTED; generalise nothing to them.** Full working at CR-E05 §1.3; source corrected at CR-E03 §1. **The lesson worth more than the fact: this hazard had propagated library-wide from a single file, and it took a verification cycle auditing a *different* file to catch it.** Related: **part-seams can fall mid-question**, with counsel's words duplicated either side — quoting across one can render a single question as two. (CR-E03 §1, added 2026-07-28.)
9. ⚠ **REV'S SPEAKER LABELS ARE NOT EVIDENCE — infer every speaker from content.** *(New 2026-07-28, Q-14 verification + Q-01 inspection. Potentially the most damaging hazard yet found, because it corrupts attribution rather than content.)* Rev's diarisation fails **systematically** across this corpus, and worst on **Day 1**:
   - **Day 1: a witness's sworn answers carry a defence attorney's label.** Officer Chris Bagley's testimony is labelled "**Richard Novak**" — Rev merged the witness and his cross-examiner into one label. A file quoting Day 1 from labels would **attribute a police officer's sworn testimony to defence counsel.**
   - **Day 1: there is no "Sturgill" label at all**, though Judge Graf hands Hull's direct to Mr. Sturgill; the next turn is labelled "Ryan McBride."
   - **The oath is misattributed on at least three days** — to defence counsel "Kathy Nestor" on Days 1 and 2, and on Day 5 to **the witness being sworn**, which is impossible.
   - ⚠ **WIDENED 2026-08-01 (Q-11): A PARTY'S ADVOCATE'S ARGUMENT CAN CARRY THE OPPOSING PARTY'S COUNSEL LABEL.** On Day 5 the single label "**Mr. McBride**" is applied to **both the prosecutor and counsel for the media coalition** — including in adjacent turns where the judge expressly distinguishes them ("Turning to the state, and then to the media"). Elsewhere on Day 5 a **witness answer** carries defence counsel "Ms. Visser"'s label. **This is more dangerous than the Day 1 failures**, because the content of an *argument* does not announce whose it is the way a witness's *answer* does. **The hazard is not only about witness answers.** ⚠ **STRUCK 2026-08-01 (Q-59): a second claimed instance — "a defence request carries prosecutor Chad Grunander's label" — was WITHDRAWN.** It rested on a reattribution CR-E06 made **with no stated basis and against the record**, where label and content did not disagree. **That was itself a hazard-9 violation, committed inside the caveat warning about hazard 9.** **The widening stands on the "Mr. McBride" instance alone.** ⚠ **The lesson is the sharper one: this hazard is easiest to break while invoking it.**
   - **One speaker, three renderings:** counsel for the victim's family appears as "Jeff Dyer" (9×), "Jeff Nyman" (1×) and "Jeffrey Neiman". **Never resolve a Rev label to a real person.**
   **Rule: identify speakers functionally (question vs. answer, argument vs. ruling) and name a person only with a stated basis — e.g. the court addressed them by name in an adjacent turn.** Where a label and the content disagree, **the content wins.** Anything already written against a Day 1 label must be re-checked before it hardens.
8. **Over-hedging is a defect too.** Two of this project's corrections have now run *toward* the State — the Dremel identification (CR-E02 v1.2) and the chamber-case identity (Q-42) — both because a summary graded down what the record states outright. **A caveat that the evidence does not support is as much an error as a claim it does not support.** (CR-AUDIT-2026-07-28 §2.2)
